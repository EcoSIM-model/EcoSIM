2. Build the aerodynamic resistance

2.1 Geometry and wind give the neutral resistance

CalcBoundaryLayerProperties computes displacement height d, roughness length z0, and effective wind measurement height zw. For a canopy with total structural surface area index S = LeafStalkAreaAll_col / AREA3(NU), the geometry uses:

x = exp(−0.5 S),   y = 1 − x
d = h max[0, 1 − 2y/S]
ze = h max(0.05, x y)
z0 = max(0.001 m, ze, ground or snow roughness)

The canopy formulas apply only when structural area is significant and canopy height reaches the snow surface; otherwise d and ze are zero. With iFlagRaiseZ0GbyVeg on, zw is measurement height plus canopy height; otherwise it is max(measurement height, d + 2 m).

rabove,0 = max[RAM, ln²((zw − d)/z0) / (0.168 uw)]
RAM in SurfaceRadiationMod = 0.00278 h m⁻¹ ≈ 10.01 s m⁻¹

Here wind uw is in m h⁻¹, so resistance is h m⁻¹. The number 0.168 is the implementation's squared von Kármán coefficient. The later heat solver has a different floor (0.00139 h m⁻¹ ≈ 5.00 s m⁻¹). The standalone weather reader initially floors wind at 3600 m h⁻¹ (1 m s⁻¹), before subsequent forcing modifiers; the ATS interface converts its supplied m s⁻¹ to m h⁻¹. A negative Koppen code instead takes the special RAM, zero-stability path.

Geometry, wind, and neutral resistance · standalone wind · ATS wind.

2.2 Add the sheltering of taller canopies

For PFT p, UpdateCanopyProperty sums the PAR-absorption fractions of PFTs taller than hp + z0. Their sum sets the attenuation coefficient α = 2 ΣfPAR,taller. The extra resistance from the PFT top to the sink height (d + z0) is:

K = RawIsoTSurf2CanopyHScal_col
rp→sink,0 = min[RACX, max(0, h exp(α) K/α
 × {exp(−α hp/h) − exp[−α(d+z0)/h]})]
ra,0,p = rabove,0 + rp→sink,0

When the required geometry or attenuation is absent, this extra term is zero; for a PFT without active leaf/stalk area it is RACX. In this canopy module RACX = 0.0278 h m⁻¹ (100.08 s m⁻¹): the equation caps the added shelter resistance. It is not a maximum for the total resistance.

How the within-canopy factor K is calculated
Hnorm = (h−d)/z0
uh = uw ln(Hnorm)/ln[(zw−d)/z0]
K = ln(Hnorm)/[0.168(h−d)uh]

These expressions are used for Hnorm > 1; otherwise K is zero. Its dimensional role in the equations is h m⁻², despite some aliases labelling it h m⁻¹.

2.3 Correct for thermal stability during the energy solve

RIB = 1.27 × 10⁸ (zw − z0)/(uw² Ta)
Ri = max[−0.3, min(0.075, RIB (Ta − Tc))]
rtarget = ra,0,p / (1 − 10 Ri)
ra,new = max[0.00139, 0.9 rprevious, min(1.1 rprevious, rtarget)]

A colder canopy gives Ri > 0 and increases the target resistance; a warmer canopy gives Ri < 0 and lowers it. The Ri limits keep the denominator between 0.25 and 4. Thus the unconstrained target spans 4 times to one-quarter of the neutral value. The actual canopy update moves within a 0.9–1.1 envelope per iteration, subject to its floor. The first iteration uses the neutral resistance as its previous value. Here ra denotes the water-vapor resistance; each update also sets rheat = ra/0.92. The neutral 10.008 s/m minimum can therefore fall to the corrected 5.004 s/m floor under unstable conditions; these are different stages of the calculation.

3. Calculate stomatal resistance

Stomatal resistance is computed in two stages. The first ties its minimum to potential photosynthetic CO₂ demand. The second increases resistance as canopy turgor declines.

Photosynthetic minimum

rCO₂,min = fPAR,live ΔCO₂ A / (CH₂O × 3600),   if CH₂O is significant
rs,min = min[rcuticle, max(0.00278, rCO₂,min/1.56)]

PhotoActivePFT obtains CH2O from the canopy photosynthesis routines. If fixation is negligible it sets the CO₂ minimum to 1.56 times cuticular resistance. At night or without sufficient leaf area, StomatalDynamics directly sets the H₂O minimum to cuticular resistance. The divisor 1.56 converts the CO₂ resistance to H₂O resistance. RSMY_stomaCO2 sets the implemented H₂O floor at 0.00278 h m⁻¹ (about 10.01 s m⁻¹).

Turgor response inside each energy/water iteration

s = exp(−Ψturgor/RCS)
rs = rs,min + (rcuticle − rs,min) s

At zero turgor, rs reaches the cuticular maximum. With greater positive turgor it approaches the photosynthetic minimum. RCS_pft is an input trait in MPa. H2OCuticleResist_pft is converted from input seconds per meter to hours per meter during initialization.

The connection to CCPOLT. update_osmo_turg_pressure computes osmotic potential using CanopyNonstOMConc_pft, then sets Ψturgor = max(0, Ψcanopy − Ψosmotic). Consequently, the prescribed CCPOLT parameter affects latent heat indirectly through turgor and rs. It does not enter the aerodynamic resistance formula directly.

4. Turn resistance into canopy heat flux

Effective area

LAI and SAI inputs are area indices in m²/m². Native canopy leaf and stalk areas are cell totals in m²: area index × horizontal cell area. Standalone prescribed phenology and the ATS input path convert their indices to these totals before the shared area/profile calculations.

The solver does not multiply conductance by every PFT's full ground area. It computes FracGridPFTCovered (fp) and uses Aeff = fp × grid area:

fp = (active leaf/stalk areap / all leaf/stalk area)
 × min(1, 0.5 × column LAI)

If total structural area is negligible, the fallback is the PFT population fraction; if population is also negligible, it is 1/NP. This is the implemented exchange-area weighting, not simply the fraction of PAR absorbed.

Sensible heat

GH = Cair Aeff / rheat
Hin = GH (Ta − Tc)

Cair = 0.00125 MJ m⁻³ K⁻¹. The result is MJ cell⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the hourly canopy equations. No stomatal term is added to this sensible-heat resistance.

Vapor transport

Gv = Aeff/ra
X = Gv(qa − qleaf)
qleaf = qsat(Tc) exp[18 max(Ψc,−5000)/(R Tc)]

vapsat returns vapor mass concentration in metric tonnes m⁻³. Gv is m³ h⁻¹ after area scaling, not m h⁻¹.

In the vapor equations below, ra means RawH2OCanopy2Atm_pft; sensible heat uses the separate RawHeatCanopy2Atm_pft.

Intercepted water is used before transpiration

  1. Potential canopy-surface exchange is Esurf,pot = X ra/(ra + 0.0139). For positive X, this is dew/condensation and transpiration demand is set to zero.
  2. For negative X, the model first evaporates held liquid water, limited by WatHeldOnCanopy_pft, then sublimates held snow, limited by SnowOnCanopy_pft.
  3. The remaining aerodynamic demand is Xrem = X − Esurface,accepted. Transpiration is Etrans = Xrem ra/(ra + rs). It is set to zero if no connected root–canopy hydraulic path is available.

For a dry canopy with no intercepted-water exchange, the third step reduces to Etrans = Aeff(qa − qleaf)/(ra + rs). For a wet canopy the actual code uses the remaining demand, so the two vapor paths must not be independently added at their full potential rates.

LEin = 2465 (Etrans + Eliquid) + 2798.55 Esnow

The latent heats are MJ per metric tonne of water. Water mass in tonnes is numerically equivalent to liquid-water volume in m³ under the model's density convention. Separate HeatEvapSens terms carry the sensible enthalpy of the transferred water; they are distinct from the turbulent sensible flux Hin.

Temperature, hydraulic coupling, and fallback

CanopyEnergyH2OIter_func updates temperature, stability-dependent aerodynamic resistance, osmotic/turgor state, stomatal resistance, transpiration, and root water exchange in a coupled iteration. Radiation and heat storage also enter the temperature equation. The trial canopy temperature is restricted to ±5 K of air temperature. The iteration limit is 200. A rejected/divergent solution can enter HandlingDivergence; inactive canopy geometry enters HandleBareSoil. Those paths can zero exchange and reset resistance to its neutral value, so a stored resistance alone does not prove an active heat flux.

5. Partition the ground area before combining paths

Let A be horizontal cell area, s = FracSurfSnowCovered_col, and l = FracSurfByLitR_col. Snow is an overlay on the soil/litter footprint. Litter cover is conditional on the ground footprint before the snow partition; l and s do not sum to one. The implementation uses products of fractions for their overlap, without resolving a spatial map of which patches snow covers.

s = min[1, √(SnowDepth / 0.075 m)],   snow-free fraction = 1−s
l = 1−FracSurfBareSoil_col

PartLitSoilFractionM calculates l from the litter thermal-presence test, litter carbon per area and mobile surface water. It can update each surface substep; s is set by the existing hourly surface partition.

Thus the direct atmospheric heat/water branches use areas A(1−s)(1−l), A(1−s)l and As. Snow-covered soil and litter are not additionally exposed to air through their snow-free heat/vapor branches. Internal soil–litter–snow heat and water transfers remain in their own balance equations.

Gas exchange has a second ground-cover definition

Soil gas transport uses b = FracAsExposedSoil_col and e = FracEffAsLitR_col = 1−b. With thermally significant litter/surface material, the current code subtracts a normalized mobile-water footprint from the geometric bare fraction:

u = min[1, max(0, mobile_surface_water / surface_water_capacity)]
b = max(0, (1−l)−u),   e = 1−b
Without thermally significant surface material: b = 1, e = 0

Consequently e can exceed l. It is the effective litter/free-water cover for the soil gas network, not a replacement for geometric litter area in every process. Its four gas-path fractions are (1−s)b, (1−s)e, sb and se, also summing to one. Atmospheric dissolution into litter water still uses the geometric footprint Al; it has a different concentration endpoint from the soil gas pool.

Two rules: add resistances along one path; add area-weighted conductances across parallel paths. An area fraction already included in a coefficient must not be applied a second time by its consumer.

6. Ground heat and vapor: local air endpoints

SurfaceResistances constructs the neutral within-canopy resistance RawIsoTSurf2SinkZ_col, using αg = 2(1−FracSWRad2Grnd_col) and a 0.0139 h/m cap on that within-canopy term. The local floor is RAM = 0.00139 h/m. The above-sink atmospheric resistance is excluded from the following neutral bases:

rsoil,0 = max(RAM, RawIsoTSurf2SinkZ_col)
rsnow,0 = rsoil,0
rlitter,0 = rsoil,0 + ResistanceLitrSurf
ResistanceLitrSurf = 0.0139 h/m = 50.04 s/m

Soil, litter and snow each calculate Ri using the selected air temperature and their own surface temperature, then update their own local resistance with a 0.8–1.2 previous-value envelope and RAM floor. Litter pore diffusion is a separate material resistance used in the gas network; it is not added to the bare-soil heat branch.

Canopy-free endpoint. When canopy_exposed_col is false, canopy area is negligible or the canopy is buried beneath snow. CalcBoundaryLayerProperties sets this shared flag using total live/dead leaf-plus-stem area greater than ZEROS and CanopyHeight_col ≥ SnowDepth−ZERO; it also gates the within-canopy aerodynamic terms. SurfaceExchangeResistance adds the atmospheric segment to each local resistance. This segment uses the existing above-sink stability expression and its 0.8–1.2 neutral-value bounds, evaluated at current reference-air and patch temperatures. Thus rexchange,j = rlocal,j + ra,j for soil, litter and snow; with a canopy, rexchange,j = rlocal,j. Vapor adds its separate evaporation resistance afterward. The implicit thermal coefficients use this assembled conductance and retain their frozen-resistance derivatives.

Their litter counterparts contain A Δt(1−s)l × XNPR; snow counterparts contain A Δt s × XNPS. Heat prefactors also contain Cair. Δt is dts_HeatWatTP in hours; XNPR/XNPS scale inner litter/snow steps (0.99999 divided by their counts). Kv has units m³ and KH MJ/K; resulting transfers are water mass or MJ per cell per substep, accumulated over the hour.

Each surface has its own vapor concentration, temperature and water-availability limits. At different gradients, the model sums their actual fluxes; a single harmonic resistance cannot reproduce all heat/vapor branches without retaining those different driving states. Evaporation is donor-limited; snow liquid evaporates before dry snow sublimates with its distinct latent heat.

7. Gas exchange: parallel patches, one atmospheric segment

The gas coefficients connect soil gas or the litter-water interface to reference atmosphere. Let ra = RawTAtm2CanopySinkZ_col be the shared canopy-air-to-atmosphere resistance. For gas i, use local bare-soil resistance rb, litter aerodynamic/film resistance rl,i, snow-surface aerodynamic resistance rs, litter pore resistance pl,i, and snow pore resistance ps,i.

CalcSnowBNDResistance uses column-mean snow depth. For s > 0, ps,i/s recovers the covered-patch pore resistance. Define rsnow,i = rs + ps,i/s. Do not evaluate that division for s = 0.

The local g is in m/h per full ground footprint; its weights sum to one. With a canopy, ra is shared and is added once after mixing. Bare soil bypasses litter pores. At full snow the snow-surface film replaces the snow-free soil/litter films, while any underlying litter pores remain. At partial cover, a nearly impermeable snow patch does not close the snow-free bypass. Separate topsoil pore diffusion remains in the fast transport consumer.

Without canopy area, replace rb, rl,i and rs in the patch formulas by their full reference-air paths: add ra,soil, ra,litter and ra,snow, respectively, using each current patch temperature. Set the additional shared ra outside the parallel mixture to zero. The same rule applies to the litter-water interface below. The molecular litter film is separated before atmosphere is added, so species scaling does not multiply the atmospheric segment.

The litter-water interface uses its own footprint

glitter,i = (1−s)/rl,i + s/rsnow,i
GasXferCoeffLitr2Atm_col(i,M,NY,NX)
 = A Δt lM / (ra + 1/glitter,i)

Here glitter,i is normalized per litter footprint, and the coefficient includes AlM, not full area A. The aqueous-side DFGcc uses the same saved FracSurfByLitRM_col(M,NY,NX) before the two interface coefficients are coupled. Both coefficients include their timestep factors and have units m³. Liquid diffusivity, solubility and the donor-water cap remain in the slow consumer. Zero litter area gives zero litter atmospheric transfer without 0/0. Litter pores are not added to this surface-water endpoint; they remain in the underlying soil-gas path.

The soil and litter coefficients connect different pools to atmosphere separately; they do not create a new prognostic shared gas concentration at canopy air. Their full-area and litter-area normalizations must therefore stay explicit.

Species dependence belongs to molecular components

qi = DH₂O/Di
pl,i = qi pl,H₂O,   ps,i = qi ps,H₂O
rl,i = rl,aero + ki rl,film,H₂O

The explicit litter film is separated from the current H₂O litter resistance in its neutral proportions: rfilm,H₂O = rl,current × ResistanceLitrSurf/(rsoil,0+ResistanceLitrSurf), and rl,aero is the remainder. This assumes the existing bounded H₂O stability multiplier acts on both terms together and preserves the H₂O total exactly. Bare-soil and snow local resistances, including their RAM floor, retain their aerodynamic treatment; no separate molecular film was introduced for them.

Shared aerodynamic terms, including ra, are not multiplied by gas diffusivity ratios. Litter/snow pore resistances keep their material-temperature scaling; the common TEFGASDIF(T) cancels from the species ratio at the same material temperature. The eight physical gases use the current diffusivity parameters; banded NH₃ uses the NH₃ surface coefficient.

Why assemble after every surface substep M?

Local stability resistances, surface cover and snow state can change during the surface solve. UpdateSurfaceGasConductancesM runs after each surface substep updates them and stores both gas coefficients with dimensions (gas,M,NY,NX). Transport later reads the corresponding M rather than reusing the final substep's coefficient. The fast handoff passes the stored species coefficients and applies its gas-cycle time factor. These hourly work arrays are reconstructed before transport and need no persistent restart field.

ResistGrndGas2CanAir_col = 1/g_ground,H₂O and CondGasXSurf_col = 1/(r_a + 1/g_ground,H₂O) remain scalar H₂O-reference diagnostics. Actual gas exchange consumes the species-indexed arrays.

8. Effective canopy air feeds back on ground exchange

For a column with canopy area, SetCanopyProperty predicts TKQ_col and VPQ_col from the completed hour's resistance-weighted sensible and latent exchange stores. It uses frozen-state derivatives to stabilize that prediction:

xq = [xa − (F−D xref)/C] / (1+D/C)

For temperature, C = Cair × cell area, F = TSHX_col, and D = dSensHeatdTKQ_col. For vapor concentration, C = latent heat × cell area, F = TLEX_col, and D = dLatHeatdVPQ_col. Without a usable slope/reference pair, the code falls back to xq = xa − F/C. Water-limited surface branches contribute zero local latent derivative when their donor limit is binding.

Canopy exchange also adds flux × effective above-sink resistance to Air_Heat_Sens_store_col and Air_Heat_Latent_store_col. The sensible store uses the heat resistance, while the latent store uses the water resistance. Ground fluxes use local resistances against TKQ/VPQ, and their feedback weights contain the separate atmospheric segment. This is why changes to canopy and surface resistances can influence the next ground-surface air state as well as their immediate flux.

For canopy-free columns, TKQ_col=TairK_col and VPQ_col=VPA_col. Ground heat/vapor uses current reference-air endpoints through the full path in section 6. Its surface fluxes do not enter the lagged canopy-air stores, and the corresponding feedback derivatives are zero. The air endpoint is selected from current canopy geometry each hour, including fallow columns with retained PFT slots.

Standing-dead canopy exchange

Exposed standing-dead area is published from hourly canopy preparation alongside live area. It contributes to aerodynamic shelter, effective projected exchange area, radiation absorption and precipitation interception. Effective height uses exposed live/dead area weights. Raw leaf/stem surface area is not added directly to projected grid area.

RootWaterUptake processes passive dead-only PFTs even with zero living population. PassiveCanopyExchange uses the existing PFT temperature, dry capacity cpo*gOC_to_m3_OM(live represented C + dead shell C), and tissue/intercepted water/snow capacities counted once. A fully buried canopy retains its material and temperature without direct atmospheric exchange.

E_new − E_old = Rn + H + LE + Q_vapor,enthalpy + Q_precipitation
C_new = C_dry + cpw(V_tissue + V_liquid,new) + cps V_snow,new

The passive one-hour solve brackets temperature between 100 and 400 K and fails if no energy-balance root is bracketed; it does not clip a trial temperature into that range. Longwave emission, stability, evaporation and donor limits are evaluated at the accepted temperature. Evaporation/sublimation cannot exceed intercepted liquid/snow plus that hour’s intercepted precipitation. Condensation adds surface liquid. Vapor mass carries sensible enthalpy in addition to latent heat.

Passive exchange does not call stomatal, canopy-osmotic or root-pressure solvers. It sets transpiration and root-water uptake to zero. Biological flags are unchanged. Its accepted heat/water fluxes and stored energy enter the existing column ledgers and remain visible in history with zero living population. New complete checkpoints retain inactive PFT thermal/water state; exposed dead area is rebuilt hourly.

A prescribed slot with zero living population must have LAI=0; SAI and HTOP then prescribe standing-dead stem area and height. Positive-population slots keep the live-canopy interpretation. Dead-only slots have no prescribed live roots or living sapwood. Existing standing-dead biomass initialized through WTSTDI supplies dry heat capacity; no separate heat-capacity input is required. The SatePhenolStandingDead example demonstrates this input contract. Mixed live/dead PFTs retain one shared temperature. Both live and dead stem thermal carbon use the same outer-shell cap: min(available C, 0.0025*total stem surface area/StemSpecVolume). Dead carbon inside that shell remains in the C/N/P biomass pools but is not activated as canopy thermal mass. Total stem area retains shell capacity under snow burial; exposed area controls exchange. This does not change the living tissue-water storage relation. Heat and intercepted-water exchange use combined exposed area; stomatal demand uses the live area fraction. Effective cover uses min(1, 0.5*(CanopyLeafArea_col + exposed dead area)/ground area), partitioned by each PFT’s exposed live-plus-dead surface share. The inherited 0.5 coefficient is an area-to-cover approximation. The live leaf term is unchanged from the zero-dead formulation; adding dead area no longer switches on all live stems in that term. Uptake records the PFTs producing live exchanges before management. Extraction uses that hourly record even if growth or removal subsequently sets population or activity to zero, and accumulates retained-slot C/N/P balance diagnostics independently of population. This is a lumped-temperature approximation, not a separate dead-wood temperature or a buried-tissue-to-snow conduction model. Dry-material changes enter the represented thermal inventory at the current PFT temperature through the existing stored-energy-change ledger. Local solver closure does not certify the full ecosystem energy budget or all mortality/turnover transfers.

Prescribed canopy exposure and height

PrescribePhenologyInterp rebuilds column height from the current PFT heights with positive prescribed leaf/stem area. A zero-area tall slot does not set aerodynamic height. Per-PFT heights and prescribed total leaf/stem areas remain unchanged.

During hourly canopy preparation, SetCanopyProfile excludes fully snow-buried PFTs from LeafStalkAreaAct_pft and the combined active area. Radiation fractions, new precipitation interception and uptake eligibility consume that active area. This is a whole-PFT burial test with the existing height tolerance; it does not introduce a fractional exposure model for partially buried crowns.

PlantUptakeAPISend and the full-model SendPlantColumnInputs load the current column’s canopy_exposed_col into the plant workspace. RootWaterUptake requires that flag as well as the PFT’s exposed area and existing biological eligibility before the atmospheric heat/water solve. Prescribed PFT existence uses total area, so burial reaches the zero-exchange fallback without discarding retained plant water. Both exposure decisions use hourly prepared geometry rather than changing independently during ground substeps. Standing-dead material uses a separate passive thermal/wet-surface path, independent of biological activity and living population.

Buried heat capacity: burial removes atmospheric exchange area, not material heat capacity. The prescribed fallback uses the actual biomass/water capacity already computed for the PFT and retains its accepted temperature, rather than using uninitialized full-model shoot mass or resetting temperature to snow without a heat transfer. This remains a lumped thermal approximation: it adds no buried-tissue conduction to snow and no separate exposed/buried temperature. A moving-snowline two-store treatment would need conservative transfer of both material and stored energy.

Energy closure and the vanishing-canopy limit

Current patch resistance enters both the surface sensible flux and its frozen implicit thermal coefficient. Canopy-free patches use the complete path to reference air; canopy-present patches use the local path to diagnosed canopy air. The atmospheric segment must not be inserted into the local stored resistance and then added again by the endpoint helper.

The vegetated-to-bare transition is a discrete branch. Finite direct bare-surface exchange does not guarantee continuity when canopy area or snow burial changes the exposure flag: the canopy-present branch uses an hourly lagged shared air endpoint, whereas the canopy-free branch uses current reference air and patch-specific stability. A common exchange formulation with a consistent vanishing-canopy limit would be needed to establish continuity. See the limiting-case explanation.

Matched local flux/temperature corrections do not establish a simultaneous global closure. Canopy-present air prediction is hourly and partially linearized. Litter latent heat is recorded in HeatEvapAir2Surf_col but omitted from the wrapper’s HeatNet2Surf_col; the global HeatErr_test threshold is bypassed by an unconditional cycle in BalancesMod.F90. See energy closure and accounting scope and the ground flux ledger.
