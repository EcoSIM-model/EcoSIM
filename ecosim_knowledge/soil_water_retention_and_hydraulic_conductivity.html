1 · Retention sets the pressure; conductivity sets the mobility

Retention: ψm(θ)

Given liquid water content, diagnose negative soil matric potential. Field capacity, wilting point and porosity anchor a piecewise curve.

Conductivity: Kψ(θ)

Sample that curve at 100 moisture classes. Weighted inverse-suction sums give a relative conductivity, scaled by vertical or horizontal saturated conductivity.

The active implementation is an anchored, piecewise logarithmic retention relation plus a discrete conductivity construction. The routines do not evaluate a van Genuchten–Mualem formula. The conductivity builder cites Green and Corey (1971) in its source comments; this guide documents the executed sums rather than substituting a general textbook equation.

  1. readimod reads soil inputs and converts their units/domain basis.
  2. StartsMod initializes anchor potentials, porosity and cold-start water/ice states.
  3. Hour1Mod rebuilds geometry, SOC/texture summaries and hydraulic properties when iResetSoilProf_col requests a disturbance refresh.
  4. SoilHydroProperty sets curvature and builds HydroCond_3D.
  5. Hydrology, surface exchange, drainage and root uptake select entries from that table.

Porosity and organic matter

fOM = clip[CSOC × 10⁻⁶ / 0.55, 0, 1]

φ = 1 − ρbulk / ρparticle   (ordinary soil branch)

The organic fraction here is an organic-matter mass fraction, obtained from carbon using the model’s 0.55 C/OM ratio. During a flagged property refresh, surface-layer logic can retain a larger previous porosity; the subsurface routine also has an explicit exceptional-density fallback. This is the normal mixture relation, not a description of every numerical branch.

4 · The retention curve

Use positive suction magnitudes ps = |ψs|, pf = |ψf| and pw = |ψw| in the logarithms. The returned matric potential remains negative. For the ordinary soil pathway:

θ < FC:
  ψm = max{ψhy, −pf exp[ln(FC/θ) ln(pw/pf) / ln(FC/WP)]}
FC ≤ θ < φ − Δθ:
  u = ln(φ/θ) / ln(φ/FC)
  ψm = −ps exp[ur ln(pf/ps)]
θ ≥ φ − Δθ:   θ is set to φ;   ψm = ψs

Defaults/constants are ψs = −0.0005 MPa, ψhy = −2500 MPa and Δθ = DTHETW = 10⁻⁶. FC and WP anchor the curve at ψf and ψw for consistent ordered parameters. The dry branch is log-linear with slope magnitude ln(pw/pf)/ln(FC/WP). With r = 1, the wet branch is also log-linear; changing r bends only the interval between FC and porosity.

The SOC thresholds for curvature are different from the 250000 threshold used by the FC/WP and Ksat estimators. The retention routine itself uses the supplied θ and parameters; it has no wetting-versus-drying scanning-curve state.

The dry bound used by callers

θairdry = exp{ln FC + [ln pf − ln(2500)] ln(FC/WP) / ln(pw/pf)}

CalcSoilWatPotential bounds working θ between this dry value and φ. The hourly soil call uses a small positive water-content floor of 10⁻⁶. A direct call to ComputePsiMCM still needs positive θ for its logarithms; a zero liquid store is handled by the caller’s bound, not by evaluating ln(0).

5 · Conductivity is built as a 100-class table

Let k = 1 be the saturated end and k = 100 the driest stored class. The builder evaluates ComputePsiMCM at each θk, retaining the routine’s near-saturation and dry-pressure limits.

Sk = 1 − (k − 1)/100   ;   θk = φ Sk   ;   ψk = ψm(θk)
B = Σm=1..100 (2m − 1)/ψm²
Ak = Σm=k..100 [2(m − k) + 1]/ψm²
Krel,k = Sk1.33 Ak/B
Kψ,v,k = Kψ,sat,v Krel,k   ;   Kψ,h,k = Kψ,sat,h Krel,k

At k = 1, A1 = B and S1 = 1, so the stored coefficient is exactly its saturated scale. The denominator is a full-curve normalization: changing FC, WP, an anchor potential or r can alter conductivity across many classes. Vertical and horizontal coefficients share the same relative shape; anisotropy enters through their saturated scales.

Runtime lookup

k(θ) = max{1, min[100, 101 − INT(100 θ/φ)]}

For nonnegative θ, INT truncates toward zero. At exact S = 0.50 the intended class is 51, corresponding to θ = 0.50φ; S = 0.499 selects class 52, corresponding to 0.49φ. Values below 0.01φ are clipped to class 100. Floating-point representation can affect exact class boundaries. The driest stored class is 1% relative saturation, not zero water, and its stored K is generally positive. A positive table entry does not create flow from an empty donor: separate water-availability constraints apply.

The builders are discrete weighted sums of inverse squared pressure. Source shorthand about integrating 1/ψ should not replace the actual ψ² denominator or its index-dependent weights.

7 · How the coefficients enter the calculation

Ψ = ψmatric + ψosmotic + ψgravity
Gsd = 2 Kψ,sKψ,d / (Kψ,sℓd + Kψ,dℓs)
Qpotential = Gsd(Ψs − Ψd) A Δt   [m³]

Hydrology selects moisture classes during substeps. Saturated/unsaturated interface branches may instead use the auxiliary wetting-front water store or cap an interface water content at ThetaSat. Surface soil K can be multiplied by the existing rain-energy reduction. Accepted transfers are then bounded by donor water and receiver space; a conductivity curve alone is not a complete infiltration, drainage or timestep model.

Hourly root uptake uses HYCDMicP4RootUptake_vr = 0.5 × [Kψ,h(θ) + Kψ,v(θ)]. Litter–soil exchange uses the corresponding vertical entries from both stores. The ordinary soil table builder does not multiply its relative curve by an additional explicit ice-impedance function; freezing affects liquid content, available storage and other flow constraints, while pond pressure and macropore formulas have their own treatment.

8 · Litter, pond water, macropores and ATS

Litter is layer zero

UpdateLiterPropertz derives litter porosity from its water-holding capacity and dry volume, sets FC = 0.5φ, WP = 0.125φ and r = 1. LitterHydroproperty uses the same weighted-sum table construction, sets horizontal litter entries to zero and builds the vertical entries. Its initial vertical saturated coefficient is 10 × 0.098. The legacy switch changes its conductivity-based threshold but preserves its r = 1 curvature.

Pond and frozen-water pressure

Ambient layers with little soil mass but nonzero pore volume call ComputePSIPond. That routine uses ice-dependent FC/WP surrogates (FCI × ice fraction, WPI × ice fraction), with a temperature-based pure-ice branch. Do not substitute the ordinary-soil retention explorer for that pressure calculation. The common soil table builder still calls ComputePsiMCM; ambient pond pressure and table construction are distinct code paths.

A separate macropore coefficient

The micropore table is not HydroCondMacP_vr. The latter is constructed from a 0.5 mm pore radius, an integer pore count inferred from macropore volume and geometric layer volume, and water viscosity at soil temperature:

n = INT[Vmacro/(π rpore² Vgeometric)]
Kmacro = 3600 π n rpore⁴ / [8 μ(T)]

These are the literal geometry/viscosity assignments in the source. They do not make macropore flow equivalent to the retention-derived micropore curve, and the presence of a coefficient does not establish which transport branches are enabled.

External hydrology coupling

ATS has additional property assignments, including externally supplied porosity and FC/WP settings in its advance module. This page’s input-estimation pipeline describes standalone EcoSIM. Check the coupling adapter before interpreting externally forced θ or parameters with these defaults.

For ordinary soil, consistent inputs require 0 < WP < FC < φ and 0 < |ψs| < |ψf| < |ψw|. The curve’s parameters, domain basis and dry bound matter as much as a soil-texture label. The explorer reproduces these equations and the 100-class construction; it does not simulate water redistribution, freeze–thaw, plant uptake or boundary management.

Verification: 400 table samples covering the three default SOC curvature branches and the legacy policy were compared with the compiled GNU Fortran 13.4.0 constitutive routines. Pressure, vertical/horizontal conductivity, dry bound and air-entry thresholds agreed within a maximum relative difference of 4.4 × 10⁻¹⁵. Retention anchors, lookup boundaries, invalid-input handling, reset behavior and desktop/mobile browser layouts were also checked. These checks validate this explanatory implementation; they do not establish scientific accuracy for a particular soil or site.
