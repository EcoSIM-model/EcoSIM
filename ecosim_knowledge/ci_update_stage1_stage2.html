How Ci is updated in stage 1 and stage 2

Code-based note · 26 November 2026 · Source: EcoSIM working tree at ba408150 plus uncommitted edits; line numbers refer to that checkout. Companion to Ci/Ca and transpiration feedback.

Summary. Stage 1 fixes the internal CO₂ as Ci* = FCO2 · Ca and derives stomatal resistance from it. Stage 2 solves a new Ci on every leaf from a balance between supply (diffusion) and demand (carboxylation). Its leaf resistance is built from the rate at Ci*, though, so for an unclamped leaf Ci* is the solution exactly.
  • C4: water stress and N/P limits scale supply and demand by the same factor, so Ci departs from Ci* only when the leaf resistance hits a bound (cuticular at low light, RCMN at high rates).
  • C3: water stress scales conductance by RS/RSL but carboxylation by √(RS/RSL). C3 Ci therefore falls below Ci* under turgor loss; this is the one physiological route by which stage-2 Ci differs from Ci*.
Stage-2 Ci is only diagnosed (DynCi2CaRatio_pft) and never feeds back to stage 1.

1 · Where the two stages sit in the hour

  1. WATSUB → AdvancePlantHydraulics (stage 1). UptakesMod calls StomatalDynamics (UptakesMod.F90: lines 204, 1598, 1652). This sets Ci*, runs reference photosynthesis at Ci*, and returns the canopy minimum stomatal resistance. The canopy energy–water solve then adds turgor stress and computes transpiration.
  2. Soil substeps. Root uptake from stage 1 is applied.
  3. PlantModel → ComputeGPP (stage 2). Called from PlantBranchMod.F90:921. For every leaf group (node × layer × inclination × azimuth × sunlit/shaded) it sets leaf resistance and iterates Ci to fix carbon.
  4. ExitPlantBalance. The leaf-area-weighted mean stage-2 Ci is diagnosed as DynCi2CaRatio_pft (PlantBalMod.F90:763). Nothing reads it back.

2 · Stage 1: Ci fixed by the PFT ratio

All in StomatesMod.F90.

Ca = CO2E − 8.33×10⁴ · Fnet · rb,CO2 / nair, clamped to CO2E ± 200   (lines 267–268)
Ci* = FCO2 · Ca   (LeafIntracellularCO2_pft, line 277)
ci* = Ci* · solubility;   ΔC = nair (Ca − Ci*)   (aquCO2Intraleaf_pft, DiffCO2Atmos2Intracel_pft, lines 950–952)
Per node, evaluated at ci*: CO₂-limited rate Vc(ci*) and carboxylation efficiency ε(ci*)   (C3: lines 456, 472; C4: lines 588, 605)
Canopy: RSX = fPAR · ΔC · Area / (CH2O · 3600)   (line 1069)
rs,min,H2O = min(rcuticle, max(RSMY, RSX/1.56))   (CanopyMinStomaResistH2O_pft, line 1074)

The canopy energy–water solve then applies turgor stress: rs = rs,min + (rcuticle − rs,min)·exp(−Ψturgor/RCS). Ci is a parameter here; it sets the conductance that delivers the reference carbon gain at that fixed drawdown.

3 · Stage 2: per-leaf Ci solve

In PhotoSynsMod.F90: C3 in ComputeGPP_C3 (lines 120–200), C4 in ComputeGPP_C4 (lines 300–400). For each leaf group with PAR > 0:

V0 = min(Vc(ci*), J(PAR)·ε(ci*)) · N   (N = RubiscoActivity_brch for C3, NutrientCtrlonC4Carboxy_node for C4)
RS = min(rcuticle,CO2, max(RCMN, ΔC / V0))   (RCMN = 15.6 s m⁻¹, PlantBGCPars.F90:235)
RSL = RS + (rcuticle,CO2 − RS) · S   (S = Stomata_Stress = exp(−Ψturgor/RCS))
GSL = nair / RSL
Iterate from Ci = Ci*:
  demand VL(Ci) = min(Vc(ci), J·ε(ci)) · N · W
  supply VG(Ci) = (Ca − Ci) · GSL
  stop when |VL − VG| / (VL + VG) < 0.005, otherwise Ci ← Ca − (0.95 VG + 0.05 VL)/GSL

The water-stress factor W on demand differs by pathway:

4 · Why Ci* is the stage-2 solution

Let s = RS/RSL (1 without turgor loss, < 1 with it). If RS is not clamped, RS = ΔC/V0. At Ci = Ci*:

supply = (Ca − Ci*) · nair / RSL = (ΔC / RS) · s = V0 · s
demand = V0 · W
CaseWdemand / supply at Ci*Result
C4, any turgor, any N/Ps1Ci = Ci*. The loop exits on its first check, so the C4 δ is exactly 0.
C3, no turgor loss (s = 1)11Ci = Ci*
C3, turgor loss (s < 1)√s1/√s > 1Ci < Ci*: demand is cut less than conductance, so Ci is drawn down
Any, RS at cuticular bound (low light)—< 1Ci > Ci*: conductance exceeds what the rate needs
Any, RS at RCMN (very high rate)—> 1Ci < Ci*: conductance is capped

N/P limitation cancels in every case: the same factor N enters V0, and so RS, and the loop's demand. For C3 under stress, the new Ci satisfies VL(Ci)/V0 = √s · (Ca − Ci)/(Ca − Ci*). How far it drops depends on how steeply VL responds to Ci: little when light-limited, more when CO₂-limited.

Assumption to verify. The argument requires that stage 2 sees the same Ca, nair, Ci*, node rates and efficiencies as the last stage-1 call. These are stored plant-photo state set by the last StomatalDynamics call in the hour. Any state that changes between WATSUB and PlantModel would add a small, non-physiological δ.

5 · Evidence from Ne1 maize (C4), 2017

From the lagged-filter test (ne1_run/experiments/ci2ca_filter_test_20261125T203548Z; report section 8), filter off, FCO2 = 0.4:

  • δ = DynCi2CaRatio − FCO2 is 0.34 at 05:00, 0.14 at 06:00 and 0.065 at 07:00, under 0.01 from 09:00 to 15:00, and 0.13–0.44 at 18:00–19:00.
  • In the dawn and dusk hours, stomatal resistance is 400–2700 s m⁻¹, near the cuticular bound. This is the clamp case in section 4.
  • Midday δ ≈ 0.004 comes from clamped shaded leaves within the leaf-area-weighted mean.

This matches the C4 analysis: δ records when the bounds bind, not physiology. A filter x(t) = α·XS + (1−α)·x(t−1) on this signal acted as a raised FCO2 set by dawn conditions, and ratcheted upward without a daily reset.

6 · Options if Ci is to become dynamic

These options respect EcoSIM's design: no prescribed A–gs–VPD relationship (no Medlyn- or Prentice-type closure). None has been tested.

  1. Make C4 match C3: use √(RS/RSL), or another non-stomatal factor, for C4 mesophyll demand. C4 Ci would then fall under turgor loss, through hydraulics alone. Check first why C4 uses RS/RSL; this is a design question, not an obvious bug.
  2. Feed back only the physiological δ: update a lagged Ci/Ca only from leaves whose RS is not clamped. For C4 this is identically zero today, so it only helps after option 1 or for C3.
  3. Base stage-2 conductance on something other than V(Ci*), for example a hydraulically limited conductance from stage 1. The stage-2 Ci would then carry independent information.

7 · Questions for later

  • Is the C3/C4 asymmetry in W (√s vs s) deliberate? Check the ecosys history and documentation.
  • How large is C3 δ under real turgor loss? Check DynCi2CaRatio_pft for soybean 2022 at Ne1 and at a drought site.
  • Does any state change between the last StomatalDynamics call and ComputeGPP (section 4 assumption)?
  • Stage 1 applies one ratio to the whole canopy; stage 2 diagnoses per leaf group. Should a dynamic ratio be per PFT or per leaf class?
