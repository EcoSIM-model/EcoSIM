EcoSIM process walkthrough

Canopy energy, transpiration,
and the ground below

EcoSIM solves vegetation temperature and water potential together: radiation and vapor exchange set water demand, root hydraulics supplies water, and tissue storage closes the balance. The ground and vegetation are advanced sequentially within each hour.

1 · The hourly calling sequence

WATSUB(yearIJ,…,PlantHydraulicModel) calls StageSoilWaterEnergy once. Staging prepares surface fractions, precipitation, radiation and canopy air, refreshes soil hydraulic properties, and rebuilds the initial transport state. The supplied PlantHydraulicModel(yearIJ,…) then calls RootWaterUptake to solve canopy temperature and water potential and publish accepted hourly root water and donor heat.

WATSUB next runs NPH heat–water substeps. UpdateSoilMoistTempM applies matching fractions of the current-hour root water and donor heat while updating water volumes, heat capacity and soil temperature. The normalized fractions sum to one; the accepted hourly exchange arrays remain available to ATS for its own soil update. At exit, working soil water and temperature are committed to the native state.

Enabled microbial processes follow hydrology. In full plant mode, PlantModel restores each column’s retained hydraulic state, refreshes soil and microbial inputs, then performs RootChemicalUptake and growth. SP mode completes its plant water/energy exchange through PlantHydraulicModel and skips plant chemical uptake and growth. Canopy longwave and resistance-weighted air feedback retain their hourly carryover.

Two different air boundaries: plant energy and transpiration use ambient TairK_col and VPA_col, mapped into the plant API as TairK and VPA. Ground sensible/vapor exchanges use TKQ_col and VPQ_col: diagnosed canopy air with canopy present, reference air with canopy absent. These variables should not be treated as interchangeable.

Sources: EcoSIMAPI.F90 (Run_EcoSIM_one_step); WatsubMod.F90 (watsub, StageSoilWaterEnergy, UpdateSoilMoistTempM); PlantHydraulicMod.F90; PlantMod.F90; PlantColumnTransferMod.F90.

The equations below follow the code’s one-hour vegetation update. Hourly rates and their one-hour increments have identical numerical values. In a general timestep formulation, multiply rates and conductances by Δt before adding them to water or heat storage. Ground substeps explicitly use dts_HeatWatTP.

3 · Vegetation energy balance

Radiation and aerodynamic exchange

Radiation derives snow cover from retained PFT snow and the current exposed live/dead geometry before current-hour interception. For exposed physical area A and retained SWE volume V, effective radiative area is A + bV; the multiplier applied to leaf, live-stem and dead-stem profiles is 1 + bV/A. The column effective LAI and PFT longwave shares use the same snow-added areas, including different PFT snow classes. See canopy radiation modeling for units, the hourly sequence and the complete area partition.

F = εcan σ fPAR A sOTC
Lemit,one side = F Tc⁴
Rn = SWabs + (LWsky + LWground) fPAR − 2 F Tc⁴
∂Rn/∂Tc = −8 F Tc³

FTHRM is F; FracPARads2Canopy_pft is fPAR; EMS_Modify_Scalar_col is the canopy emissivity modifier. The factor two represents the two emitting sides in this canopy energy expression. One-side emission is accumulated into downward canopy longwave LWRadCanG_col.

GH = cair Aheat / rheat,   cair = 1.25 × 10⁻³ MJ m⁻³ K⁻¹
H = GH (Ta − Tc)

r_heat = RawHeatCanopy2Atm_pft = RawH2OCanopy2Atm_pft / BndlResistH2OToHeatRatio, with BndlResistH2OToHeatRatio=0.92 from the Chilton–Colburn analogy. Vapor uses the water resistance and effective water area. Canopy CO₂ boundary transport separately uses BndlResistCO2ToH2ORatio=1.34; this is distinct from stomatal gas scaling.

The common target is max(r_min, r_neutral/(1−10 Ri)). The live iteration limits each update to 0.9–1.1 times its previous iteration value while approaching that target; the passive scalar solve evaluates the target directly at each trial temperature. Both use the same Richardson clipping and physical lower bound. Ground surfaces retain their separate resistance treatment.

Water carries both latent heat and sensible enthalpy

LE = Lv(e + eliq) + Lsesnow
Qprec = (cwPrain + csPsnow) Ta
Qadd = Rn + LE + Qvapor,enthalpy + Qprec − Qroot,out

Evaporation/transpiration and sublimation make LE negative. HeatEvapSens is a separate water-mass enthalpy term: condensation carries cpw × TairK; liquid loss carries cpw × TKC1; snow sublimation carries cps × TKC1. Root uptake has negative CumPlantHeatLoss2Soil, so subtracting it adds imported heat to vegetation. Heat capacities for transported liquid and snow are volumetric, MJ m⁻³ K⁻¹.

The temperature candidate actually used

Let T₀ = TKCX be the start-of-update vegetation temperature, C₀ its initial heat capacity, Cnew the capacity after trial water changes, and D = −dRadNetdT ≥ 0. In one-hour integrated notation, the code computes:

Tcandidate = [C₀T₀ + DTtrial + Qadd + GHTa] / [Cnew + GH + D]
Cnew = C₀ + cw(Prain + eliq + e − q) + cs(Psnow + esnow)

Radiation and its derivative are evaluated at the current trial temperature. Each candidate step is limited to Ttrial ± 5 K, then relaxed. This step limit does not bound the final departure from air temperature. Accepted live solutions are checked for heat closure; a failed coupled solve uses the conservative passive energy solver.

The relaxation factor uses XC, initially 0.5, and a base rate of 0.5 or 0.1 depending on heat capacity. Direction reversals halve XC; water-potential updates can reset it. These are internal vegetation-solver controls, separate from the restored litter and snow outer stabilizers.

Source: UptakesMod.F90, CanopyEnergyH2OIter_func and UpdatePlantWaterVars.

4 · From vapor gradient to transpiration

In this section ra denotes the water-vapor resistance RawH2OCanopy2Atm_pft, and Aeff is the effective water exchange area.

ρleaf = vapsat(Tc) exp[18 max(Ψc, −5000) / (RGASC Tc)]
EX₀ = (Aeff/ra)(ρair − ρleaf)
esurface,potential = EX₀ ra / (ra + rleaf surface)

The exponential is reproduced in the code’s MPa, kelvin, and RGASC convention. More negative water potential suppresses leaf vapor concentration; warming increases the saturation concentration. The leaf-surface resistance here is ResistLeafSurf, distinct from stomatal resistance.

Condensation: EX₀ > 0

Water is assigned to dew/liquid gain. The residual demand for transpiration is set to zero. The code records this in QdewCanopy_pft and VapXAir2CanopyLiq_pft.

Water loss: EX₀ ≤ 0

Potential surface loss first evaporates intercepted liquid, limited by WatHeldOnCanopy_pft, then sublimates intercepted snow, limited by SnowOnCanopy_pft. Their accepted losses are subtracted from EX₀ to obtain the remaining transpiration demand.

EXremaining = EX₀ − (eliq + esnow)
e = EXremaining ra / (ra + rs)

For a dry canopy, positive outward transpiration E = −e simplifies to:

E = Aeff(ρleaf − ρair) / (ra + rs)

This dry-canopy formula does not include interception partitioning. The actual wet-canopy branch uses the sequential allocation above. Transpiration and root uptake are coupled through water potential and tissue storage, rather than enforcing transpiration equal to root supply at every trial.

Hydraulic stress closes stomata

rs = rmin + (rcuticle − rmin) exp(−Ψturgor/RCS)
Ψturgor = max(0, Ψc − Ψosmotic)

Lower turgor raises resistance toward the cuticular limit. StomatalDynamics supplies the photosynthesis-related minimum resistance; CalcStomataResist4H2O applies the current turgor response. Thus temperature, vapor demand, water potential, and photosynthesis affect one another.

Sources: UptakesMod.F90; PlantMathFuncMod.F90 (CalcStomataResist4H2O, update_osmo_turg_pressure).

5 · Root supply, redistribution, and tissue storage

qnl,potential = [Ψc + Ψgravity − Ψsoil,l] / Rsoil+root,nl
qnl = max[−Wavailable,l fnl, min(qnl,potential, Vair,l fnl)]

Index n identifies a root/mycorrhizal category; l identifies a soil layer. Categories and rooted layers contribute in parallel. Individual pathways combine soil resistance with radial and axial root resistance; the axial routing can include a medium-root branch. The code uses total soil water potential supplied to the plant API and a gravitational adjustment for effective canopy height.

Negative q draws soil water into vegetation. Positive q permits hydraulic redistribution into soil, subject to pore space. A second check scales positive releases if canopy water, intercepted stores, and simultaneous uptake cannot support them. Root-water heat uses the donor temperature: soil temperature for uptake, vegetation temperature for release.

Qroot,out = Σq>0 cwTcq + Σq≤0 cwTsoil,lq

Water potential determines a target tissue-water store

F(Ψc) = 0.16 + 0.10|Ψc|/(0.05|Ψc| + 2)
Wtarget = 10⁻⁶ MC/F(Ψc)
DIFFZ = Wtarget − Wb,old
DIFFU = e − q
DIFFW = DIFFU − DIFFZ

get_FDM describes F as a dry-mass/water ratio, while the executed storage expression supplies the carbon variable CanopyMassC4H2OStorage. The equation above records that implementation without silently inserting a dry-mass conversion. The factor 10⁻⁶ converts grams of water to its equivalent volume under the code’s density convention.

If root supply exceeds transpiration, e − q is positive and tissue can rehydrate. Otherwise tissue water can supply some loss. The solver adjusts Ψc using estimates of storage and flow sensitivity until this supply–storage residual is small.

Sources: UptakesMod.F90 (CalcPlantHydroResistance, root loop and storage residual in CanopyEnergyH2OIter_func); PlantMathFuncMod.F90 (get_FDM).

6 · Iteration, acceptance, and state update

  1. Initialize canopy storage-associated carbon, heat capacity, hydraulic resistances, trial potential, and intercepted-precipitation heat.
  2. For each trial, update longwave emission, aerodynamic resistance, osmotic pressure/turgor, stomatal resistance, vapor exchange, latent heat, and the temperature candidate.
  3. Under-relax vegetation temperature. When its candidate mismatch is below 0.05 K, or every tenth iteration, evaluate root flows and the tissue-water residual.
  4. Use storage/flow sensitivities to adjust water potential. Relative water residual below 0.005 triggers a final stomatal update/confirmation sequence. A small potential increment after at least 30 iterations or the maximum iteration count provides alternative exits.
  5. Commit TKCanopy_pft into TKC_pft; update tissue/interception stores in UpdatePlantWaterVars; aggregate fluxes in EXTRACTs and export the plant column.
Wb,new = Wb,old + e − q
Wheld,new = Wheld,old + Prain + eliq
Wsnow,new = Wsnow,old + Psnow + esnow

The final canopy heat capacity is reconstructed from storage-associated organic matter, tissue water, intercepted liquid, and intercepted snow. HeatStorCanopy_pft is assigned T0*C0 − Tc*Cnew + HeatEvapSens + PrecpHeatbyCanopy; it should not be read as simply CΔT or as an extra ground heat transfer.

Termination is not a guarantee of a small energy residual. The code clips the temperature candidate, relaxes its update, has alternate water-potential exits, and calls HandlingDivergence at the iteration limit. That fallback modifies fluxes and states. Inactive/hydraulically inactive vegetation has a separate HandleBareSoil path.

7 · What vegetation sends to the ground

There is no single “canopy heat flux into soil” output containing all exchanges. Radiation, air feedback, root advection, and precipitation follow different variables and timing. PlantUptakeAPIRecv publishes hydraulic results before soil substeps; the later full-model PlantAPIRecv publishes chemical and growth updates.

Standing-dead canopy exchange

Exposed standing-dead area is published from hourly canopy preparation alongside live area. It contributes to aerodynamic shelter, effective projected exchange area, radiation absorption and precipitation interception. Effective height uses exposed live/dead area weights. Raw leaf/stem surface area is not added directly to projected grid area.

RootWaterUptake processes passive dead-only PFTs even with zero living population. PassiveCanopyExchange uses the existing PFT temperature, dry capacity cpo*gOC_to_m3_OM(live represented C + dead shell C), and tissue/intercepted water/snow capacities counted once. A fully buried canopy retains its material and temperature without direct atmospheric exchange.

E_new − E_old = Rn + H + LE + Q_vapor,enthalpy + Q_precipitation
C_new = C_dry + cpw(V_tissue + V_liquid,new) + cps V_snow,new

The passive one-hour solve brackets temperature between 100 and 400 K and fails if no energy-balance root is bracketed; it does not clip a trial temperature into that range. Longwave emission, stability, evaporation and donor limits are evaluated at the accepted temperature. Evaporation/sublimation cannot exceed intercepted liquid/snow plus that hour’s intercepted precipitation. Condensation adds surface liquid. Vapor mass carries sensible enthalpy in addition to latent heat.

Passive exchange does not call stomatal, canopy-osmotic or root-pressure solvers. It sets transpiration and root-water uptake to zero. Biological flags are unchanged. Its accepted heat/water fluxes and stored energy enter the existing column ledgers and remain visible in history with zero living population. New complete checkpoints retain inactive PFT thermal/water state; exposed dead area is rebuilt hourly.

A prescribed slot with zero living population must have LAI=0; SAI and HTOP then prescribe standing-dead stem area and height. Positive-population slots keep the live-canopy interpretation. Dead-only slots have no prescribed live roots or living sapwood. Existing standing-dead biomass initialized through WTSTDI supplies dry heat capacity; no separate heat-capacity input is required. The SatePhenolStandingDead example demonstrates this input contract. Mixed live/dead PFTs retain one shared temperature. Both live and dead stem thermal carbon use the same outer-shell cap: min(available C, 0.0025*total stem surface area/StemSpecVolume). Dead carbon inside that shell remains in the C/N/P biomass pools but is not activated as canopy thermal mass. Total stem area retains shell capacity under snow burial; exposed area controls exchange. This does not change the living tissue-water storage relation. Heat and intercepted-water exchange use combined exposed area; stomatal demand uses the live area fraction. Effective cover uses min(1, 0.5*(CanopyLeafArea_col + exposed dead area)/ground area), partitioned by each PFT’s exposed live-plus-dead surface share. The inherited 0.5 coefficient is an area-to-cover approximation. The live leaf term is unchanged from the zero-dead formulation; adding dead area no longer switches on all live stems in that term. Uptake records the PFTs producing live exchanges before management. Extraction uses that hourly record even if growth or removal subsequently sets population or activity to zero, and accumulates retained-slot C/N/P balance diagnostics independently of population. This is a lumped-temperature approximation, not a separate dead-wood temperature or a buried-tissue-to-snow conduction model. Dry-material changes enter the represented thermal inventory at the current PFT temperature through the existing stored-energy-change ledger. Local solver closure does not certify the full ecosystem energy budget or all mortality/turnover transfers.

Sources: UptakesMod.F90, ExtractsMod.F90, CanopyHydroMod.F90, and passive conservation tests.

Prescribed canopy exposure and height

PrescribePhenologyInterp rebuilds column height from the current PFT heights with positive prescribed leaf/stem area. A zero-area tall slot does not set aerodynamic height. Per-PFT heights and prescribed total leaf/stem areas remain unchanged.

During hourly canopy preparation, SetCanopyProfile excludes fully snow-buried PFTs from LeafStalkAreaAct_pft and the combined active area. Radiation fractions, new precipitation interception and uptake eligibility consume that active area. This is a whole-PFT burial test with the existing height tolerance; it does not introduce a fractional exposure model for partially buried crowns.

PlantUptakeAPISend and the full-model SendPlantColumnInputs load the current column’s canopy_exposed_col into the plant workspace. RootWaterUptake requires that flag as well as the PFT’s exposed area and existing biological eligibility before the atmospheric heat/water solve. Prescribed PFT existence uses total area, so burial reaches the zero-exchange fallback without discarding retained plant water. Both exposure decisions use hourly prepared geometry rather than changing independently during ground substeps. Standing-dead material uses a separate passive thermal/wet-surface path, independent of biological activity and living population.

Buried heat capacity: burial removes atmospheric exchange area, not material heat capacity. The prescribed fallback uses the actual biomass/water capacity already computed for the PFT and retains its accepted temperature, rather than using uninitialized full-model shoot mass or resetting temperature to snow without a heat transfer. This remains a lumped thermal approximation: it adds no buried-tissue conduction to snow and no separate exposed/buried temperature. A moving-snowline two-store treatment would need conservative transfer of both material and stored energy.

8 · The hourly canopy-air feedback

The predictors below apply only while canopy_exposed_col is true: total live/dead canopy area exceeds the column threshold and canopy height is at least SnowDepth−ZERO.

Tqn+1 = Tan+1 − SHn / (cairA)
ρqn+1 = ρan+1 − SLn / (LvA)
SH = HgroundRground + ΣpHpRp

These are the unstabilized hourly diagnostic forms. Both temperature and vapor concentration now use resistance-based corrections when matching references and nonzero slopes are available. They do not integrate an explicit canopy-air heat store. Negative resistance-weighted sensible heat means vegetation/ground has heated air, increasing the next diagnosed Tq. Tq stays fixed while ground substeps advance within that hour.

Accepted litter and snow thermal updates carry their boundary heat corrections into matching flux ledgers. Snow’s inner semi-implicit sensible correction is included once; its outer reconstruction retains additional longwave treatment. Litter retains its outer longwave amplification.

Implemented temperature stabilization: resistance estimate. Each active soil, litter, and snow exchange adds its step-integrated sensible conductance to SurfSensHeatConduct_col. After the full hourly heat is assembled, RedistMod::UpdateCanopyAirDerivatives applies the same above-canopy resistance weight used for surface sensible heat.
KH = Rabove Σactive ground steps,j cairAexchange,jΔtj/ra,j
Tqn+1 = [CATan+1 − SHn + KHnTqn]/[CA + KHn],   CA = cairA

The conductance sum is reset after the previous-hour predictor is consumed, then rebuilt from actual active ground substeps. This includes the proper snow/litter subcycling factors and changing aerodynamic resistance. Plant sensible heat uses ambient Ta, so its conductance is not added as a direct Tq derivative. Its complete accepted heat remains in SH.

dSensHeatdTKQ_col stores the weighted derivative; TKQHeatRef_col stores the matching completed-hour temperature. Both are saved in restarts; legacy restarts use zero derivative for the first resumed predictor and rebuild it during that hour. Zero conductance recovers the explicit predictor.

The estimate holds surface temperatures, resistances, and other state/forcing fixed when taking the derivative. It does not propagate the implicit surface-temperature response, phase changes, or indirect plant responses. It changes only the next boundary-temperature predictor and leaves completed physical heat transfers unchanged. There is no finite-difference ΔT threshold in the resistance-based slope calculation.

Both predictors use the explicit solution below, with x = Tq or ρq, F = S_H or S_L, C = c_air A or L_v A, and D = cfeedback × K_H or cfeedback × K_V:

x_new = [x_a − (F − D x_old)/C]/[1 + D/C]

cfeedback is a compile-time multiplier shared by both predictors: 0 recovers the explicit diagnostic, values between 0 and 1 weaken stabilization, and 1 uses the full frozen-state estimate. Stored derivatives remain unscaled. The vapor slope sums active latent-energy derivatives: L_v Cd for unconstrained soil/litter or liquid-snow exchange, L_s Cd for sublimation, and zero when the relevant donor limits are active. dLatHeatdVPQ_col and VPQLatHeatRef_col are saved together in restarts; missing fields recover the explicit first predictor.

Separate scalar predictors freeze heat–humidity cross-responses. They impose no saturation relationship between Tq and ρq. Their lag and frozen-surface slopes can affect mean conditions; numerical stability alone does not demonstrate absence of temperature or humidity bias. See the heat–humidity predictor assumptions.

Sources: SurfPhysMod.F90 (SetCanopyProperty); SurfLitterPhysMod.F90 (UpdateLitRBe4RunoffM); SnowPhysMod.F90 (UpdateSnowPack1M).

Air endpoint follows the hourly exposed-canopy diagnostic. When canopy_exposed_col is false because canopy area is negligible or the canopy is buried beneath snow, TKQ_col=TairK_col and VPQ_col=VPA_col. Each soil, litter and snow branch uses SurfaceExchangeResistance to add its current patch-specific atmospheric resistance once. Stored local resistances remain local. With canopy present, the same helper returns only local resistance and the hourly predicted canopy air remains the endpoint.

The vegetated-to-bare transition is a discrete branch. Finite direct bare-surface exchange does not guarantee continuity when canopy area or snow burial changes the exposure flag: the canopy-present branch uses an hourly lagged shared air endpoint, whereas the canopy-free branch uses current reference air and patch-specific stability. A common exchange formulation with a consistent vanishing-canopy limit would be needed to establish continuity. See the limiting-case explanation.

Air-endpoint selector and full-path assembly: SurfaceAirExchangeMod.F90. See also surface energy closure and exchange-area partition.
