Retained canopy snow and radiation

Canopy snow is distinct from snowpack layers. Radiation uses each PFT’s retained intercepted SWE and current exposed physical area. The added interception area is snow-area coefficient × retained SWE volume, in m² per cell. Leaf, live-stem and dead-stem profiles share the dimensionless multiplier 1 + added area / exposed physical area. The same snow-added areas set column optical depth and PFT longwave fractions. Buried material retains its snow but contributes zero atmospheric radiative area. Current-hour interception and unloading follow radiation preparation. See canopy radiation modeling for the shared parameters, cover equation and hourly sequencing.

1 · A layered store of water and heat

EcoSIM represents a snow column with up to JS layers. Each layer contains dry snow, retained liquid water and ice. Snowfall and rain enter at the top; water, vapor and heat move vertically; the bottom exchanges with litter and soil. Horizontal redistribution exports material from the top snow layer to neighboring cells.

Snow water equivalent (SWE) is a water-mass measure expressed as liquid-water depth. It is not snow depth: a porous pack can be much thicker than its SWE.

2 · State variables, geometry and units

For a layer, write S for dry-snow water-equivalent volume, W for liquid volume, I for physical ice volume, and T for temperature in kelvin. These are grid-scaled volumes; divide by horizontal cell area A to obtain depths. Array metadata sometimes uses the model’s d-2 notation; the equations below keep the cell area explicit.

Surface snow cover is diagnosed as fsnow = min[1, √(D / Dmin)], assuming nonnegative depth. It controls the area exposed to snow-specific radiation and exchange. It is not a separate snow mass pool.

3 · One model hour contains nested snow steps

  1. Prepare the hour. Weather and canopy interception supply throughfall. StageSnowModel copies layer states into working arrays and resets cumulative changes.
  2. Loop over NPH soil substeps. Surface physics prepares snow states and calls SolveSnowpackM for a numerically resolved top store; the complementary precipitation fallback initializes new snow.
  3. Loop over NPS snow subcycles. Compute atmospheric exchange, vertical liquid/vapor/heat transfers, a semi-implicit phase predictor and updated temperatures. Record the accepted sensible and linearized longwave transfers in the same heat ledgers.
  4. Complete local physics for every grid cell. Then construct accepted lateral snow transfers from the post-local donor states, with a shared donor limit.
  5. Gather and apply the snow redistribution. Watsub accumulates net face transfers; UpdateSnowAtM updates snow states. Water/heat outputs are available to the receiving litter and soil paths.
  6. Transport and end-of-hour maintenance. Non-salt and salt transport routines use stored substep faces and the matching thermal donor criterion. SnowMassUpdate refreshes diagnostics and compaction; SnowpackLayering transfers material between layers.
Δtsoil = dts_HeatWatTP = 0.99999 / NPH   [h]

Δtsnow = Δtsoil × XNPS

dts_wat and dts_sno are also used as numerical transfer/relaxation factors. They should not be substituted indiscriminately for the physical snow-step duration. The factors 0.99999 mean these implemented subdivisions are slightly smaller than exact reciprocals.

4 · Atmospheric water and energy exchange

PartitionPrecip routes rain over the snow-covered fraction and snowfall throughfall into the snow input. Canopy snow unloading is included in snowfall throughfall. Each snow subcycle receives its allocated precipitation and heat.

QSW,abs = (1 − asnow) QSW,in
QLW,out = KsnowT1⁴ / NPS
Ksnow = εsnow σ A fsnow Δtsoil
Qrad = QSW,abs + QLW,in − QLW,out

Here the radiation quantities Q are step-integrated energies, not instantaneous W m⁻². Shortwave and incoming longwave are already scaled for the snow subcycle. Unless prescribed albedo is enabled, the surface albedo is a volume-weighted mixture with coefficients 0.85 for dry snow, 0.30 for ice, and 0.06 for liquid, including current precipitation. The current OTC emissivity modifier applies to canopy emission only; snow keeps its ordinary emissivity.

Fv,pot = Gv[vair − vsat(T1)]
Fwater = max(Fv,pot, −max(W1 dts_sno, 0))
Fsub = max[min(Fv,pot − Fwater, 0), −max(S1 dts_sno, 0)]
Qlatent = LvFwater + LsubFsub
QH,0 = KH(Tq − T0)

Tq is the selected air temperature TKQ_col; vapor exchange uses VPQ_col. These are diagnosed canopy-air values with canopy present, or reference-air values with canopy absent. The above sensible expression is the initial evaluation; the temperature solve below supplies the accepted transfer.

Positive atmospheric vapor transfer adds water; negative values remove it. Loss first draws from liquid, then from dry snow by sublimation. The implemented positive branch adds condensation to liquid. Advected sensible heat uses the donor temperature: snow temperature for loss, selected air temperature for gain. Radiation, latent heat, sensible heat and advected heat all enter the surface snow energy budget.

Air endpoint follows the hourly exposed-canopy diagnostic. When canopy_exposed_col is false because canopy area is negligible or the canopy is buried beneath snow, TKQ_col=TairK_col and VPQ_col=VPA_col. Each soil, litter and snow branch uses SurfaceExchangeResistance to add its current patch-specific atmospheric resistance once. Stored local resistances remain local. With canopy present, the same helper returns only local resistance and the hourly predicted canopy air remains the endpoint.

For canopy-free snow, SurfaceExchangeResistance adds the atmospheric segment using the current inner snow temperature, bounded against the neutral atmospheric resistance. The snow sensible coefficient K_H below uses that full path. The local snow resistance is retained separately; no second shared atmospheric segment or lagged canopy-air feedback is applied. Where canopy is present, the existing local resistance and diagnosed air endpoint remain in use.

4a · A semi-implicit surface-temperature balance

At each inner step, SnowAtmosExchangeMM updates the local snow aerodynamic resistance, assembles the complete resistance to the selected air endpoint, then freezes that resistance and air state for the temperature solve. Its sensible coefficient KH includes snow-covered area and inner-step duration. SolveSnowpackM also linearizes emitted longwave about the old top-snow temperature T0.

rsnow = SurfaceExchangeResistance(NY,NX,T0,RawH2OSnow2CanAir_col)
KH = AScaledCdHOverSnow_col / rsnow   [MJ K⁻¹]
KH = (1.25 × 10⁻³) A fsnow Δtsnow / rsnow
KL = 4 LWEmscefSnow_col T0³ / NPS   [MJ K⁻¹]
KB = KH + KL
T′ = (C0T0 + Q0 + QFT + KB T0) / (C′ + KB)

Here rsnow is in hours per meter and Δtsnow is in hours. Q0 is the net heat initially evaluated using T0, including atmospheric and internal transfers; C′ is recomputed from the accepted final constituents. Only the top layer receives these atmospheric derivatives; interior layers have KB = 0. The longwave derivative is formed when provisional capacity exceeds ZEROS.

QH,accepted = QH,0 − KH(T′ − T0) = KH(Tq − T′)
QLW,out,accepted = QLW,out,0 + KL(T′ − T0)
C′T′ = C0T0 + Qaccepted + QFT

Both accepted boundary terms are propagated into the face, accumulated snow heat and atmospheric diagnostic ledgers. The outer UpdateSnowPack1M reconstruction consumes those accepted transfers and retains its existing additional longwave linearization; it adds no second sensible stabilizer. When a layer is exhausted, the inner solve can still finish its accepted boundary balance if C′ + KB > 0. A temperature assigned to a zero-capacity store carries no stored energy.

KB is a boundary-response coefficient, not extra snow mass or a physical soil heat capacity. Soil and litter receive heat through their existing conductive, advective and residual-transfer paths. This implementation does not solve snow, litter, soil and local-air temperature as one simultaneous coupled system. With canopy present, the hourly air predictor retains its partial sensible derivative with accepted surface temperatures held fixed. With canopy absent, reference air is prescribed directly and that predictor is bypassed.

5 · Liquid drainage, vapor and conduction

A layer’s unconstrained liquid-drainage estimate retains liquid equal to 5% of dry-snow water equivalent:

Qliq,pot = max[0, WL − 0.05 max(0,SL)] × dts_sno
Qadv,liq = cwTdonor Qliq

Within the pack, the code further caps drainage using AirFilledFracSnow of the receiving layer. That variable is an air-filled fraction; this page does not reinterpret it as a dimensional receiving-pore volume. At the bottom, separate routines partition discharge and exchange between litter, soil micropores and soil macropores using receiving conditions.

Vapor exchange follows the saturation-vapor concentration difference, a conductance combining layer diffusivities and thicknesses, an equilibrium-air-volume bound, and a donor-liquid bound. Its transported heat includes sensible energy plus latent heat. Heat conduction follows the temperature difference with an equilibrium-energy cap.

ρ̂bulk = min[0.6, (S + W + ρ̂iI) / Vbulk]
ksnow = 0.0036 × 10(2.650 ρ̂bulk − 1.652)
G12 = 2 k1k2 / (k1d2 + k2d1)
Qcond,pot = G12(T1 − T2) A fsnow Δtsnow

The conductivity coefficient is in the model’s MJ–hour–meter system. Snow density therefore affects insulation as well as depth and compaction. Net layer transfers are the difference between incoming and outgoing face quantities; they are accumulated before the phase calculation.

Source: SnowPackIterationMM, SnowSurLitterExch and SnowTopSoilExch

6 · Freeze–thaw is source-limited and relaxed

SolveSnowpackM forms an apparent temperature from the pre-step sensible energy, provisional post-transport capacity and the same boundary derivative used in the final temperature solve. If it is above the freezing point with solids present, melting is allowed; if below freezing with liquid present, freezing is allowed.

Tapp = (E0 + Q0 + KB T0) / (C* + KB)
κ = 1 + Tfreeze(cw − cs) / Lf ≈ 2.7156
QFT,pot = (C* + KB)(Tfreeze − Tapp) × dts_wat / κ
−Lf(S + ρ̂iI) ≤ QFT ≤ LfWavailable

C* is the working capacity formed at the phase test; all accepted dry-snow, liquid and ice transfers have already entered its constituent estimates. The Kirchhoff factor κ accounts for the difference in sensible heat capacity between liquid and dry snow in the code’s energy convention. The lower and upper bounds apply in the respective melting and freezing branches. LtHeatIceMelt = 333.55 MJ per m³ WE. Negative QFT consumes sensible heat while melting; positive values release heat while freezing.

Melting is divided between dry snow and ice according to their water-equivalent shares. Let ms and mi denote melted water-equivalent volumes; freezing gives a negative mi and creates ice.

S′ = S + ΔStransport − ms
W′ = W + ΔWtransport + ms + mi
I′ = I + ΔItransport − mi / ρ̂i
T′ = (E0 + Q0 + QFT + KB T0) / (C′ + KB)

The density factor in the ice update is essential. Pure phase change preserves S + W + ρ̂iI. The relaxation factor and source bounds mean this is not an instantaneous clamp of every mixed layer to 0°C. Positive final mass residuals are retained. Freezing still includes small-liquid-reserve and tiny phase-flux tolerances; an empty store without a remaining boundary response uses a placeholder temperature.

Source: SolveSnowpackM / UpdateSnowPack1M

7 · Lateral routing uses a shared donor budget

For donor cell i, the driving elevation is zi + Di. A lower neighboring surface can receive a fraction of the donor’s top-layer constituents. The implemented proposal is a slope-and-distance rule, not a wind-speed transport law.

gif = [(zi + Di) − (zj + Dj)] / ℓf
qif = min[1, max(0,gif) Δtsoil / max(1,ℓf)]
αi = 1 / max(1, Σoutgoing faces qif)
(FS,f, FW,f, FI,f) = αiqif(Si,1, Wi,1, Ii,1)
FE,f = Ti,1(csFS,f + cwFW,f + ciFI,f)

The code requires donor depth above MinSnowDepth. This formula retains the code’s empirical numeric meter/hour scaling; it should not be read as a dimensionally complete constitutive transport law without its implied calibration. Face distance is indexed at the eastern/southern cell: backward flow uses the current cell’s shared-face index.

One scale factor applies to every outgoing direction and every constituent. A donor can export at most its existing top layer. Incoming snow is retained for the next substep rather than funding additional exports immediately. The receiving cell gets exactly the accepted donor transfer, including its heat. A positive thin receiving store reconstructs its temperature from the combined sensible energy and capacity, including on the final outer substep.

Xi′ = Xi + Σ Fincoming − Σ Foutgoing,   X ∈ {S,W,I}
Ei′ = Ei + Σ FE,incoming − Σ FE,outgoing

SnowDepth_col in this gradient remains the existing hourly depth diagnostic. The routing pass uses post-local-physics donor constituents and temperatures; it does not introduce substep updates of the depth diagnostic. Accepted faces are stored in DrySnoFlxByRedistM_2DH and the corresponding water, ice and heat arrays. Hourly totals and downstream tracer routines consume those accepted faces.

8 · Compaction, relayering and disappearance

Compaction changes snow density and depth rather than directly removing water. UpdateSnowLayerL increases density through temperature-dependent metamorphism and overburden compression. Its compression term uses accumulated snow water equivalent, horizontal area and a temperature/density-dependent viscosity.

Δρ̂met = ρ̂s × 10⁻⁵ exp(0.04 T°C)   when ρ̂s < 0.25
η = 0.25 exp(−0.08 T°C + 23 ρ̂s)
Δρ̂load = ρ̂s Voverburden,WE / (A η)

These are the code’s end-of-hour empirical density increments; there is no extra snow-subcycle duration multiplying them here. Fresh snowfall also changes the surface density. SnowpackLayering then moves fractions of constituent volumes, sensible heat and associated chemical pools between adjacent layers to satisfy layer-volume bounds. Splitting or merging is a transfer between stores, not new snowfall or melt.

When the top snow store is at or below the retained layer-maintenance threshold and reference-air temperature TairK_col is above freezing, UpdateSnowAtM transfers its residual liquid and solids to litter or the topsoil pathway. Dry-snow water equivalent is converted to physical ice volume through DENSICE. SnowpackDisapper provides additional end-of-hour disappearance handling, including ATS output arguments.

Native residual transfers add liquid to liquid and retain dry snow and ice as physical ice: ΔIreceiver = I + S/ρ̂i. They transfer the donor sensible energy with the material, recompute receiver capacity and temperature, and preserve occupied lower snow layers. Litter organic heat capacity is computed from organic-matter volume using gOC_to_m3_OM, plus its water and ice contributions. Optional coupling outputs are initialized and assigned independently when present; external coupling applies its own receiving-state contract.

Elayer = CbeforeTbefore
Tafter refresh = Elayer / Crecomputed   for Crecomputed > 0
Treceiver,new = (Ereceiver,old + Etransferred) / Creceiver,new

The hourly refresh retains each positive layer’s stored sensible energy. It introduces no temperature reset to reference air and no compensating external adjustment to HEATIN_lnd for that retained store. Clearing the transferred top store leaves the lower-layer mass, temperatures and energy intact; aggregate snow diagnostics are recomputed.

To check mass closure, sum S + W + ρ̂iI over snow layers and receiving litter/soil pools, then account for precipitation, atmospheric loss and external boundaries. Internal phase changes and internal redistribution cancel in that total. For heat closure, include radiation, latent exchange, sensible exchange, precipitation/advection and phase-change heat consistently; temperature alone is insufficient when capacity changes.

Timestep sensitivity

Four Lake runs with 30 outer steps per hour and 20, 40, 80 or 160 snow subcycles all complete 20 days. Maximum differences between hourly snow temperatures are:

The large threshold-reset differences are reduced in the tested trajectory. The remaining maxima do not decrease monotonically, so these runs establish neither strict temporal convergence nor long-term scientific correctness. These timestep tests do not establish a simultaneous snow/ground/air energy solve. The canopy-present air value is predicted hourly; canopy-free snow uses prescribed reference air through its complete resistance.

Salt-enabled end-to-end validation remains incomplete. The allocation repair gets Lake past the original unallocated-array failure. With salt chemistry enabled, the run still stops in the first hour because the staged reaction demand exhausts dissolved aluminum and the transport retry cannot accept the remaining removal. A successful workspace test does not establish a successful salt trajectory or restart.

The vegetated-to-bare transition is a discrete branch. Finite direct bare-surface exchange does not guarantee continuity when canopy area or snow burial changes the exposure flag: the canopy-present branch uses an hourly lagged shared air endpoint, whereas the canopy-free branch uses current reference air and patch-specific stability. A common exchange formulation with a consistent vanishing-canopy limit would be needed to establish continuity. See the limiting-case explanation.

Whole-surface energy accounting also depends on the aggregate ledger limits, including the litter latent-heat omission and bypassed global heat-error threshold. Matched snow thermal corrections alone do not certify that broader budget.
