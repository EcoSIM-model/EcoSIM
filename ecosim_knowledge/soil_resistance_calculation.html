How soil evaporation resistance is calculated

The soil supplies two parallel transport pathways: diffusion of water vapor through air-filled pores and liquid transport driven by the retention gradient. Their combined internal resistance is added to the air-side resistance when forming evaporation demand.

This note explains the general standalone ordinary-soil calculation, then demonstrates it with the dryland topsoil. The dryland values are an example; the equations accept each layer’s own hydraulic parameters and current state.

1. The general resistance equation

rs = δ / 2Dv,bulk + Dl,   Dl = Br θ K g,   g = dψr/dθ

The denominator contains two transport coefficients with the same units. Adding them gives parallel transport; dividing by the half-layer distance δ/2 gives the conductance. The topsoil cell’s representative water source is taken at its center, so δ is the full control-volume thickness.

K g has units m²/h. No gravitational-head conversion is needed. B is the density ratio; the BET adsorption parameter is called C in this implementation.

rtotal = rair + rs,   Jout = ρv,source − ρv,airrair + rs

With densities in Mg/m³ and resistances in h/m, Jout is a potential outward mass flux in Mg/(m² h). EcoSIM uses the opposite sign for its integrated demand: positive is into the ground. Exposed area and timestep are applied outside rs, followed by liquid/ice partitioning and donor limits. Consequently, rs alone does not specify the accepted evaporation flux.

2. From the current soil state to resistance

  1. Read the current transport volumes. Compute θ = Vliquid,mic/Vsoil,mic and a = Vair,mic/Vsoil,mic, bounded to [0,φ]. These are the current inner-step states, not liquid content raised to an air-dry floor. In an ice-free example, a = φ−θ; the general calculation uses the supplied air volume, including ice displacement.
  2. Form the vapor coefficient.
    Dv,bulk = Dg a² τ / φ,   τ = 0.66
    The hourly free-gas diffusivity is updated as Dg = 0.077(T/298.15)1.75 m²/h and supplied to the surface routine. The resistance kernel does not add porosity or tortuosity again.
  3. Select K from the native table. The current decision is to retain the existing direct 100-class lookup for both soil and litter, with no interpolation in log(K). For nonnegative θ:
    k = max[1, min(100, 101 − floor(100θ/φ))],   K = HydroCond(3,k)
    The third direction is vertical. The table samples span S = 0.01 to 1; the lowest class is held toward zero. The lowest bin also extends up to S < 0.02 under this index rule. These tables come from the selected native hydraulic scheme.
  4. Evaluate the retention derivative and density factor. Above the wet cutoff, use the native derivative and the caller’s source-vapor density ratio. Below it, evaluate the resistance-only cubic/BET continuation described next. The default soil cutoff is θw = 0.6 θfc, or Sw = 0.6 Sfc.
  5. Sum transport and apply the distance. Compute Dl = BrθKg and rs = δ/[2(Dv,bulk+Dl)]. The kernel evaluates positive products and sums in logarithmic form to preserve the hyperdry limit without overflowing the separate factors.
  6. Add the air-side resistance and constrain the resulting flux. The surface exchange routine supplies the resistance to the chosen canopy-air or atmospheric endpoint. Snow-free area, litter coverage, timestep, and available water/ice enter outside the internal-resistance calculation.
Native derivatives and conductivity depend on the selected soil model

The same transport equation works with EcoSIM’s default anchored retention or the selected van Genuchten implementation. The usual native derivative is a bounded finite difference with h = εmachine1/3 φ, centered in the interior and one-sided at bounds. Copies protect the actual water state from the native pressure routine’s saturation adjustment. The dryland example below uses the default EcoSIM curve. Its dry-branch exponent is b = ln(|ψwp|/|ψfc|)/ln(θfc/θwp) ≈ 7.699035; its native pressure is capped at −2500 MPa.

3. The dry-end retention used inside resistance

The runtime follows three regimes. Native hydraulic pressure and the vapor driving gradient keep their existing definitions; ψr and its derivative below are local transport quantities.

Liquid contentResistance retentionHow g is obtained
θ ≥ θwSelected native ψ(θ)Native bounded derivative
θd < θ < θwCubic y = ln(−ψr/1 MPa)Analytic derivative of that cubic
0 < θ ≤ θdBET adsorption plus Kelvin relationAnalytic BET/Kelvin derivative

Cubic bridge and temperature update

t = (θ−θd)/(θw−θd),   y(t) = c₀+c₁t+c₂t²+c₃t³
ψr = −exp(y) MPa,   g = ψr(c₁+2c₂t+3c₃t²)/(θw−θd)

The four endpoint conditions match pressure and first derivative to BET on the dry side and the native curve on the wet side. The resulting pressure is monotonic under the implemented shape constraint; g itself can have a local maximum. Its slope, the second derivative of ψ, need not be continuous at a join.

η = ln(T/Tref),   (c₀,c₁,c₂,c₃)T = (c₀+η,c₁,c₂−3η,c₃+2η)ref

Tref = 293.15 K. Native wet pressure/slope stay fixed, while the BET pressure scale follows T. This update takes one logarithm and scalar arithmetic, with no root search. Material coefficients are cached after initialization and refreshed when their inputs change; restart reconstructs them from restored native parameters.

BET and Kelvin at the very dry end

θ = θm Cx(1−x)[1+(C−1)x],   ψr = A(T) ln x,   A(T) = RT/18 [MPa]

x is relative humidity, R = 8.3143 in the model’s Kelvin conversion, and θm is monolayer content on the same volume basis as θ. The numerical factor 18 follows the water molar volume 18×10⁻⁶ m³/mol and conversion of MPa to Pa. Soil C defaults to 18 in PhysPars. It is a global prior, with a note for future column-dependent values, and is not a namelist control.

f(x) = (1−x)[1+(C−1)x]
g = A f(x)²θmCx[1+(C−1)x²],   θ/x = θmC/f(x)

For ice-free, zero-osmotic-potential soil, Brθ = (ρl/ρv,sat) θ/x has a finite positive limit as θ→0⁺. The derivative grows approximately as A/θ. The evaluator works with ln g and ln(θ/x) to preserve these cancellations.

How the cold-start dry anchor and cubic coefficients are derived

First take θw = 0.6θfc, and obtain ψw, gw from the selected native curve. At Tref, start from xd = 0.3 and ψd = Aref ln(0.3) ≈ −163.027 MPa. If ψd ≥ 2ψw, use ψd = 3ψw instead and recompute xd. This preserves ordering when the prescribed wet cutoff is already very dry; it does not establish a physically calibrated adsorption capacity.

The native inverse supplies θd,0 = θnative(ψd). Define:

q = −f(xd)/{ln xd[1+(C−1)xd²]},   w = −θwgw/ψw
Δ = ln(|ψd|/|ψw|)−ln 2,   rmin = max[q/(q+Δ), 1−2Δ/w]

Omit the second rmin term if w = 0. Then choose:

θd = max[θwrmin, min(0.95θw, max(0,θd,0))]
θm = θd f(xd)/(Cxd)

The shape constraint is designed to ensure monotonicity for T ≥ Tref/2 = 146.575 K; runtime also checks the exact extrema of the cubic derivative. A very dry wet anchor can produce an unreasonable inferred θm, so these are estimation rules rather than measured adsorption parameters.

Let h = θw−θd, yd = ln(−ψd/1 MPa), yw = ln(−ψw/1 MPa), md = gd/ψd, and mw = gw/ψw. The reference Hermite coefficients are:

c₀ = yd,   c₁ = h md
c₂ = 3(yw−yd)−h(2md+mw)
c₃ = 2(yd−yw)+h(md+mw)

Preserving the caller’s source-vapor physics

The caller first computes ρv,source from native matric plus osmotic potential, or from ice equilibrium, and supplies Bcall = ρl/max(ρv,source,tiny). In the liquid-controlled dry branch, only the matric part is replaced inside resistance:

Br = Bcall exp[(ψnative,m−ψr)/A]
ln(Brθg) = ln Bcall + ψnative,m/A + ln(θ/x) + ln g

This retains any osmotic contribution already contained in Bcall. The source density in the external evaporation gradient stays unchanged. When supplied ice volume is positive and T is below freezing, the code instead keeps Br = Bcall while using the continued slope; it does not replace the ice-equilibrium source partitioning.

5. Explore the dryland calculation

Change liquid saturation, temperature, or thickness. Material retention anchors and the native K table stay fixed; temperature changes the BET/cubic evaluation, vapor density, and free-gas diffusivity consistently for this controlled example. Ice and osmotic potential remain zero.

Positive S values are limited to [10⁻¹²,1] for display; enter 0 to examine the exact-zero branch. Changing thickness scales rs linearly. The actual model receives independently diagnosed air volume; a = φ−θ is the ice-free demonstration assumption.

6. Limits and scope of verification

  • Exact zero water: Dl = 0. With open air pores, the remaining resistance is vapor-only. K = 0 also removes the liquid term. Zero thickness gives zero resistance; both transport pathways blocked return the model’s HUGE sentinel. Very small representable resistances may return zero.
  • Retained lookup artifacts: class changes can cause resistance downsteps, and the positive lowest-class K floor causes the extreme-dry behavior shown above. No log(K) interpolation or vanishing dry-tail extrapolation has been adopted.
  • Different runtime paths: pond/lake cells use their open-water treatment. In ATS coupling, the standalone porous-soil calculation is bypassed by the existing saturation-based surface resistance. An ordinary-soil API call without temperature uses the earlier dry-boundary-slope extension rather than cubic/BET.
  • Parameter evidence: C = 18, the 0.6 field-capacity fraction, and the inferred monolayer are chosen priors and construction rules. They require material-specific dry-retention evidence for calibration. The continuation matches pressure and first derivative but does not guarantee a monotonic derivative.

The committed implementation passed 19 focused tests, exact 10-day restart comparisons for dryland and biocrust, and two-year smoke runs for all 22 eligible example namelists with finite history and final checkpoint outputs. The two documented exclusions were Blodget.warming (existing SIGILL) and dryland2 (checkpoint dependency). These checks establish tested numerical behavior and run completion; conservation closure and scientific calibration were not assessed.

The two-year screen required successful exit, the full 17,520 or 17,544 hourly steps, an endpoint checkpoint dated exactly two years after each start, and clean history and checkpoint scans. The tested GNU 13.4.0 Release executable used soil/litter BET C = 18/13 and field-capacity cutoff fractions 0.6/0.5.
