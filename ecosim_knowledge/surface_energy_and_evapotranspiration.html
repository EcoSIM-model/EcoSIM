Resistance sets the rate of sensible heat and water-vapor exchange. Surface temperatures also depend on radiation, storage, phase change and transported water enthalpy. The air endpoint changes when canopy area vanishes or snow buries the canopy.

The helper assembles the complete path without changing the stored local resistances. Local ground resistance updates are limited to 0.8–1.2 times their previous working value and the RAM floor. The canopy-free atmospheric bound is instead relative to the neutral atmospheric value rₐ⁰. Richardson number is clipped to −0.3…0.075. Ground RAM = 0.00139 h m⁻¹ = 5.004 s m⁻¹; the neutral atmospheric calculation has its own 10.008 s m⁻¹ floor.

For gas transport, canopy-present paths mix local patch conductances before adding one common atmospheric segment. Canopy-free paths include their patch atmospheric segments before mixing; the additional common segment is zero. Gas molecular diffusivity scales pore transport, and Schmidt scaling applies to the explicit litter surface film. Turbulent atmospheric transport is species independent. See the gas and area equations.

4 · Vapor exchange and donor limits

ΔVⱼ,proposal = Aⱼ Δtⱼ (ρ_endpoint − ρ_surface,j)/(r_exchange,j + r_material,j)
Q_LE = L_v ΔV_liquid + L_s ΔV_sublimation

Soil and litter surface concentration depends on saturation concentration, current temperature and water potential; snow uses saturation at its current temperature. The standalone soil branch adds RZ. ATS soil evaporation can instead use the saturation-dependent surface resistance, bounded below by RZ. Snow and litter retain their own surface resistance and donor limits.

Evaporation or sublimation cannot withdraw more than the accepted available donor water. A donor-limited branch has zero frozen derivative with respect to air vapor concentration. Condensation can add water. Vapor mass also carries sensible enthalpy in addition to latent heat; those terms remain separate in the energy ledger.

5 · What energy closure means here

E_new − E_old = Q_radiation + Q_sensible + Q_latent + Q_water enthalpy
+ Q_conduction/advection + Q_phase + Q_other external

Every Q is an accepted, step-integrated transfer into the selected thermal store. Water transfers change heat capacity as well as carrying heat, so stored heat must use the accepted final constituents. Internal snow–litter–soil transfers cancel in a combined budget only when both sides record the same accepted transfer.

Local implicit boundary response

The soil solve freezes its longwave and sensible derivatives, dHGrnddTKsoi1 = −4 LWEmscefSoil T³ − CdSoiHSens. The sensible coefficient uses the complete selected-endpoint resistance. CorrectSoilSurfaceHeatM carries the accepted radiation and sensible corrections into the face and hourly ledgers.

Snow and litter likewise include boundary-response coefficients in their thermal updates. For snow, K_H = c_air A_snow Δt_snow/r_exchange,snow. An accepted temperature change δT adjusts sensible heat by −K_H δT and emitted longwave by +K_L δT. These are matched flux corrections, not extra physical snow or soil heat capacity. Snow’s outer reconstruction adds no second sensible stabilizer; its additional longwave treatment remains. Litter retains its outer longwave amplification.

Scope of the closure

The derivatives freeze resistance and other state, rather than differentiating the entire coupled hydrological and thermal system. With canopy present, the air predictor is also delayed by one hour. The implementation therefore does not solve all surface temperatures, phase states, canopy air and humidity simultaneously.

Aggregate accounting limits. SurfLitREnergyBalanceM records litter latent heat separately in HeatEvapAir2Surf_col, and includes it in the accepted litter thermal calculation, but omits it from HeatNet2Surf_col. In ModelDiags/BalancesMod.F90, an unconditional cycle bypasses the subsequent HeatErr_test threshold check. A completed run or consistent local thermal correction therefore does not certify whole-system energy closure.

For a closure diagnostic, compare accepted stored-energy changes against every external boundary term and phase term for the same domain and timestep. Use corrected face transfers once; do not add both a net flux and its component fluxes. See the surface flux ledger and snow thermal solve.

6 · Evapotranspiration combines several reservoirs

Atmospheric water loss includes soil/litter evaporation, snow sublimation, intercepted canopy liquid/snow loss and transpiration. Vegetation first allocates potential vapor loss to intercepted liquid and snow, subject to their stores. Residual demand passes through aerodynamic plus turgor-dependent stomatal resistance. Root hydraulic supply and tissue-water storage constrain the vegetation solution.

Transpiration removes water through roots in soil layers; it is not another direct withdrawal from the surface litter pool. Population, effective PFT area and grid area must not be applied again to already integrated transfers. See the canopy water calculation.

7 · Hourly air feedback when canopy is present

S_H = Q_H,ground rₐ + Σₚ Q_H,p w_H,p
S_L = Q_LE,ground rₐ + Σₚ Q_LE,p w_V,p
w_H,p = r_heat,p rₐ⁰/r_canopy⁰,p;   w_V,p = r_water,p rₐ⁰/r_canopy⁰,p

Ground uses RawTAtm2CanopySinkZ_col for rₐ. Plant feedback uses distinct heat and water weights; it does not use water resistance for both. The stores TSHX_col and TLEX_col hold resistance-weighted sensible and latent heat, in MJ m⁻¹, rather than heat in an air reservoir.

x_next = [xₐ,next − (F − D x_ref)/C]/[1 + D/C]
x = Tq: F = S_H, C = c_air A;   x = ρq: F = S_L, C = L_v A
D = cfeedback × K

F, K and x_ref come from the same completed hour. K_H is the above-canopy-weighted sum of active ground sensible conductances; K_V is the analogous latent-energy derivative, respecting donor limits and sublimation latent heat. Plant exchange uses reference air and contributes accepted heat to F, but is not a direct derivative with respect to Tq or ρq. Restart files preserve each slope and its matching reference. Missing or unusable pairs fall back to the explicit predictor x_next = xₐ,next − F/C.

With canopy absent: the predictor is bypassed, Tq = Tₐ and ρq = ρₐ, and the stored air-feedback derivatives are zero. Ground fluxes still enter ecosystem heat ledgers, but do not rebuild a lagged canopy-air feedback.

8 · Timing and accepted transfers

  1. Hourly geometry and forcing establish canopy presence, neutral resistance and the selected air endpoint.
  2. Ground physics advances soil, litter and snow in nested substeps, using current surface temperatures to assemble canopy-free atmospheric resistance.
  3. Implicit thermal updates carry accepted boundary corrections into storage and matching flux accumulators.
  4. Vegetation advances using reference air, updated soil conditions and ground longwave; root transfers and canopy emission are staged for subsequent ground preparation.
  5. REDIST updates ecosystem ledgers and, with canopy present, pairs the completed-hour air-feedback stores, slopes and reference states.

The detailed order and water/heat transfer bookkeeping are described in ground physics and canopy physics.

9 · Predictor assumptions

The current predictor uses frozen resistance-based slopes. cfeedback is a compile-time multiplier: 0 selects the explicit predictor, 1 the full estimated slope, and intermediate values weaken damping. Stored slopes remain unscaled. The calculation has no finite-difference temperature threshold.

Temperature and humidity are predicted separately. Each freezes thermal–vapor cross-responses, surface-temperature response, phase changes and changes of resistance within its derivative. No saturation relationship is imposed between the predicted air values. A fully coupled closure would instead require simultaneous residuals for heat and vapor with those responses included. Numerical stability alone does not demonstrate convergence or unbiased mean conditions.

10 · Does vegetation disappear continuously?

A continuous limit is not guaranteed. The hourly geometry test makes a discrete switch when canopy area crosses ZEROS or canopy height crosses SnowDepth−ZERO. With exposed canopy, local ground exchange uses the hourly diagnosed air node. Without exposed canopy, each patch exchanges directly with reference air through local plus atmospheric resistance, with current patch stability.

For one steady surface with fixed resistances:
H = c_air A(Tₐ − T_surface)/(r_local + rₐ)

A consistently solved intermediate air node can reproduce that series-path result. The implemented vegetated branch instead uses an hourly lagged, partially linearized predictor. With multiple patches, its shared air endpoint also differs from separate direct patch paths. The atmospheric stability reference changes at the switch. These differences can produce a flux jump when canopy area crosses the threshold; a finite bare-soil resistance does not by itself establish continuity.

A continuous formulation would need a common exchange network whose solution approaches the same bare-surface limit as canopy exchange conductance vanishes. That behavior is a design requirement for further work, not a property established by the current branch selector.
