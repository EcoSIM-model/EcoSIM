Root recruitment paid by growth.
Transport shaped by cohorts.

The updated woody-root model separates potential branching capacity from living axes. New medium roots require structural investment, compete with existing tips for elongation, and enter a young cohort with its own geometry.

01 · Architecture

Connected primary, medium and fine root classes

GPP supplies the wider plant carbon economy. Shoot–root transfers and root nonstructural C/N/P pools provide resources for maintenance and growth; the cohort routine receives actual net structural growth after the caller has paid resource and respiration costs. It does not allocate GPP directly or create an additional carbon source.

Functional classes, with partially independent growth

Primary, medium and fine roots are functional model classes. Botanical branching orders describe positions within a root branching hierarchy; EcoSIM’s three classes do not correspond directly to those orders or to a universal sequence of diameter classes. Here, young medium roots represent developing transport laterals, while the fine-root class represents absorptive roots.

Each class has its own growth and maintenance calculations. The model does not explicitly mature living fine-root axes into medium axes, or medium axes into primary axes. Young → established medium-root promotion occurs within the medium class; it changes cohort membership while preserving the class’s aggregate count, length and C/N/P. Resource remobilization through nonstructural pools is distinct from reclassifying a living root axis.

Interpretation: the formulation represents coordinated growth among persistent functional classes, with partial independence of their growth calculations. It omits explicit developmental transfers between classes. A future formulation based strictly on diameter or individual-root developmental fate would need explicit, mass-conserving reclassification rules.

Soil layers retain independent effective uptake pathways. Cytokinin produced locally can pass from fine roots through medium/primary compartments toward the shoot; deeper uptake does not flush upper-layer compartments through a serial soil-to-shoot chain.

Activation: the new medium-root recruitment/cohort formulation operates for woody vascular plants with lcoarseroot=.true. Primary-root maturity, fine-root support, maintenance, resource limitation and existing growth gates remain part of the surrounding model.
02 · Why three functional classes?

Separate the woody framework, developing transport branches and absorptive roots

The three-class representation is useful when the model must predict growth allocation, root profiles, recruitment and transport together. A coarse–fine split can describe bulk root carbon, but two undifferentiated biomass stocks provide too little information to determine all of those processes. The issue is the functions and geometry represented within the pools, rather than the number of pools alone.

Main insufficiency of a simple coarse–fine model: total woody carbon is not a sufficient measure of metabolic activity, available branching sites or transport capacity; total fine-root biomass is not a sufficient measure of absorptive capacity. Using those two stocks as universal proxies can couple processes that should respond differently.

Why accumulated lignified carbon matters

A tree can accumulate a large coarse-root structural C stock without a proportional increase in metabolically active tissue or new branching capacity. For an idealized cylindrical root of fixed tissue volume per unit C:

Cstructural = π × radius² × length / volume_per_C
At fixed length: doubling structural C increases radius by √2.
It does not, by itself, add root length, axes or absorptive tips.

The larger radius can change surface area and conducting geometry, so thickening can increase potential support. Our formulation represents that radius effect in potential medium-axis capacity, then requires separate investment to realize new axes. It does not infer a living branch count directly from accumulated coarse-root mass.

If total coarse-root C also weights growth sinks or acts as the denominator of a reserve concentration, old structural wood can dominate allocation or make reserves appear diluted. These are possible formulation errors to inspect in a two-class model; they are not unavoidable consequences of using two classes.

Lignified does not mean functionless. Woody tissue supplies mechanical support and conducting structures, and includes living cells involved in metabolism and storage. Chemical lignification, living-cell abundance and transport function are distinct properties. Excluding an entire lignified stock from every physiological calculation would also be an oversimplification. Evidence on living woody tissues and respiration.

What three classes do—and do not—resolve

For this EcoSIM application, the clearest benefit is a separate accounting unit for transport-lateral recruitment and extension, between framework thickening and absorptive-root production. That helps prevent framework C from serving as an automatic proxy for fine-root support and allows explicit competition for investment.

The medium class can itself accumulate structural carbon. Therefore, three classes do not remove the need for active-tissue fractions, consistent C/N/P budgets, realistic radii and lengths, and validated turnover and transport parameters. The classes are persistent functional groups; the current model omits explicit developmental transfers between them.

A carefully formulated two-class model can remain adequate for bulk carbon accounting, or can recover some missing behavior through active-tissue subdivisions, turnover heterogeneity and effective network geometry. Three classes are justified here by the processes we want to resolve, not by a universal requirement that every tree model have exactly three pools.

The one-year comparison below verifies investment and restart accounting. Its higher medium-root C and lower fine-root C do not establish improved biological realism or a corrected long-term shoot–root ratio.

03 · State and units

Young tissue is a subset of the total stock

For each soil layer, parent structural-root group and PFT, existing medium-root totals remain authoritative. The three new state arrays identify the young subset. Established stocks are calculated by subtraction.

Axis counts can be fractional because they represent population means. Internal population length and mass include the plants in the grid cell; per-area history outputs divide by ground area or soil volume. volume_per_C has units m³/gC, so radius is in meters. The young pools must never be added again when computing plant carbon totals.

The geometry helper bounds the young subset to nonnegative totals when deriving cohorts and returns zero radius for compartments without positive C and length. This is a numerical safeguard, not a new material transfer.

04 · Potential and investment

Capacity creates a target; growth pays for axes

1. Scale branching capacity with primary lateral surface

Npotential = branching_frequency × primary_length_per_axis × primary_population
             × max(1, primary_radius / nominal_primary_radius)
Ntarget = min(1, max(0, fine_support_activation)) × Npotential

For a cylinder of fixed length, lateral area grows in proportion to radius. The radius ratio therefore scales the length-based branching capacity relative to an unthickened reference root. Its lower bound of one preserves reference capacity for thin primary axes. A larger potential target alone creates neither living axes nor transport area.

2. Share new elongation investment between recruitment and old tips

Dnew = max(Ntarget − Ntotal, 0) × Linitial
Dextension = Ntotal × extension_rate × 1 hour
B = growth_C × max(1 − thick_fraction, 0) × specific_length
s = min(1, B / (Dnew + Dextension))    if total demand > 0; otherwise 0

recruited_length = s × Dnew
new_axes = recruited_length / Linitial
existing_extension = s × Dextension

The growth update is evaluated for positive growth_C and specific_length. growth_C is gC per grid cell for the current hourly call. The demands and budget are population lengths in meters. specific_length is the medium elongation conversion retained from the existing model: FracRootElmAllocm(C,fine_comp) × Root1stSpecLen_pft, in m/gC.

Recruitment

Every new axis receives the configured initial length and its corresponding C investment. Existing standing length cannot finance new counts. Recruitment is limited by the target deficit and the available growth budget.

Existing axes

All existing tips request extension. That extension is divided between young and established cohorts by their counts. Recruiting short axes does not directly reduce the established cohort’s length/count ratio.

3. Partition the complete structural C/N/P growth vector

radial_C = max(growth_C − (recruited_length + existing_extension)/specific_length, 0)
young_extension = existing_extension × Nyoung/Ntotal
old_young_C_share = Cyoung/Ctotal

young_growth_C = (recruited_length + young_extension)/specific_length
                 + radial_C × old_young_C_share
young_growth_fraction = clamp(young_growth_C/growth_C, 0, 1)

Etotal += growth_CNP
Eyoung += growth_CNP × young_growth_fraction

The count and carbon shares use zero-safe denominators; for a newly starting population with no axes, radial investment belongs to the young cohort. Unused elongation capacity becomes radial investment. Thus realized growth enters total C/N/P exactly once, and the young subset receives the corresponding fraction. The caller’s reserve debit and respiration accounting are not repeated inside the cohort helper.

05 · Developmental closure

Promotion transfers cohort membership, not total biomass

promoted_axes = min(Nyoung,
  max(Lyoung − Nyoung × Linitial, 0) / (Lmature − Linitial))
promoted_length = min(Lyoung, promoted_axes × Lmature)
moved_fraction = promoted_length / Lyoung

Nyoung -= promoted_axes
Lyoung -= promoted_length
Eyoung *= (1 − moved_fraction)

If all young axes are promoted, all young length moves. Total count, length and C/N/P remain unchanged; the established residual gains exactly what the young subset loses.

Approximation: this two-stage moment closure uses extension beyond the initial length to fund promotion at a maturation length. It does not track individual root age or a complete length distribution. Promoted axes mix into the established cohort and can change its mean length and radius. The model avoids immediate dilution by newly recruited axes, but does not preserve every older axis as a separate cohort.
06 · Geometry and transport

Compute each cohort first, then combine pathways

MediumTransportGeometry returns population count and length, tissue radius, lumen area per axis, a phloem geometry factor, and transport-annulus area per axis for each cohort. Transport tube depth is limited by the actual radius.

depth[k] = min(radius[k], ZSTX, max(FSTK × radius[k], nominal_primary_radius))
Agas[k] = π × (2 × radius[k] × depth[k] − depth[k]²)
Gphloem[k] = N[k] × (Agas[k]/π)
             / max(0.5 × mean_length[k], fine_root_tip_length)

phi[k] = phi_min + (phi_max − phi_min) × (1 − exp(−3 × radius[k]/R95))
Alumen[k] = coarse_root_xylem_area(dmax, radius[k]) × phi[k]

Gphloem is a geometric conductance factor, not a carbon flux. The xylem function retains the existing radius/sapwood-area formulation. The lumen fraction and cross-sectional area are evaluated at each cohort’s own radius.

Lifecycle and restart handling

Young C/N/P, length and count follow their aggregate stocks through thinning, whole-root death and upward layer relocation. Partial withdrawal retains the surrounding model’s shortening of surviving axes. Recruitment constraints do not prevent later tissue loss.

New restart files store all three young-subset arrays. Old files containing none initialize existing medium roots as established. Files containing only part of the cohort state are rejected. Both the full plant API and the earlier water-uptake API transfer the geometry inputs. New optional history fields are appended after existing registrations to preserve pointer indices in older history restart files.

Optional history profiles

  • RootMedYoungC_pvr — gC/m³ soil.
  • RootMedYoungAxes_pvr — axes per living plant.
  • RootMedYoungLength_pvr — m root/m² ground.

Add these names to the case’s existing hist_fincl1 list. They are inactive by default; existing total profiles keep their original meanings.
