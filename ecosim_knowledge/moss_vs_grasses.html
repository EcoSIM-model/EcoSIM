How moss is represented,
and how it differs from grasses

Moss shares the plant model’s carbon, nutrient and water bookkeeping. Explicit nonvascular switches change how drying affects gas exchange, growth and litter formation.

02 · Identity

Which flags make a moss?

Moss is represented as a living plant functional type (PFT). It shares the plant carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, energy, phenology and geometry machinery with grasses. Bryophyte behavior is selected chiefly by IGTYP=0, read into iPlantRootProfile_pft. The predicate is_root_bryophyte tests that exact code. The name “root profile” therefore selects more than rooting depth: it also changes photosynthesis and respiration rules.

Source: PlantInfoMod.F90:1856 · PlantMathFuncMod.F90:267

The code follows flags, not the PFT name. A record named moss… must still have the intended categorical settings. Changing IEBTYP alone does not activate the IGTYP=0 branch. IROOTJK, when used for prescribed root distributions, is a different setting.

03 · Hydrology

Moss uses effective uptake organs and canopy water stores

The model retains a soil → uptake organ → canopy → atmosphere water pathway for both groups. Water uptake is constrained by soil and organ hydraulic conductances, water availability, canopy storage, and the solved canopy potential. For moss, variables named “root,” “stem” or “xylem” are effective shared-model compartments; their presence is not evidence that biological moss is treated as a vascular grass anatomically.

A fixed parameter RTSK(IGTYP) weights primary-root tip sink strength: 0.25 for moss, 1 for profile-1 grass, 2 and 4 for deeper profile classes. It multiplies a sink expression that also depends on axis abundance and transport resistance. This biases allocation toward a shallower effective moss uptake system; it is not a hard maximum rooting depth. Uptake-organ growth, population density, carbon supply, soil layers and oxygen conditions still matter.

Source: PlantBGCPars.F90:306 · RootMod.F90:5777

The example moss62 has PORT=0, while gr3s62 has PORT=0.05. These catalog choices affect modeled gas-filled root porosity and oxygen transport. They are not automatic consequences of the moss name, and other moss records can have nonzero values.

Water held on surfaces is a separate reservoir

Rain-holding capacity is FoliarWatRetcap(IGTYP) × leaf+stem area. The coefficient is 0.00050 m for moss and 0.00025 m for profile-1 grass: at one unit of modeled leaf+stem area per ground area, capacities are 0.50 versus 0.25 mm. Incoming canopy rain is also weighted by intercepted radiation fraction. These coefficients do not define the entire moss mat’s water-holding capacity.

Source: CanopyHydroMod.F90:19 · CanopyHydroMod.F90:54

The canopy energy solver explicitly permits vapor condensation when its air-to-canopy vapor gradient is positive. It separately tracks surface-held water and plant tissue water. The existence of this shared condensation calculation does not establish a calibrated moss-specific fog/dew assimilation model.

Source: UptakesMod.F90:874

04 · Explore the equations

Same canopy ψ, different water-stress modifiers

In StagePlantForGrowth, both groups compute a response to their own canopy total water potential, ψc, in MPa:

Moss: fW = exp[0.05 max(ψc, −5000)]
Grass: fW = exp[0.10 max(ψc, −5000)]

The result is truncated downward to increments of 10−4. For example, at ψc = −20 MPa, the stored factors are 0.3678 for moss and 0.1353 for grass. At the same ψ, this modifier declines more slowly for moss. Actual moss can nevertheless dry more strongly because of its surface resistance, water supply and uptake geometry.

05 · Carbon uptake

Surface resistance replaces the vascular stomatal response

Both PFTs retain the same light interception, leaf/node geometry, Rubisco/electron-transport calculations, and carbon–nutrient feedbacks. The ICTYP flag selects C₃ or C₄; being grass does not imply C₄. In the C₃ photosynthesis branch:

StepMossGrass
Water-dependent CO₂ resistance modifierStomata_Stress = 1 in growth stagingexp(−turgor / RCS)
CO₂ diffusion resistanceRSL = RS + (Rcut − RS) × 1 = RcutRSL = RS + (Rcut − RS) × exp(−turgor/RCS)
Non-stomatal factor WFNBReceives the direct canopy-ψ growth factor abovesqrt(RS/RSL)

Source: GrosubsMod.F90:534 · PhotoSynsMod.F90:136 · PlantBranchMod.F90:846

Thus, in this growth/GPP pathway, moss uses a prescribed cuticular/surface resistance and direct desiccation response. Grass can change the stomatal resistance contribution with turgor. Shared biochemical capacities, absorbed light, N/P feedback and diffusion still determine the resulting assimilation.

The water-flux solver still calls a shared resistance function

CalcStomataResist4H2O has no explicit moss switch. It interpolates between minimum and cuticular water-vapor resistance using exp(−turgor/RCS). The example moss has RCS=100 MPa and RSMX=150 s m⁻¹; grass has RCS=0.2 MPa and RSMX=2500 s m⁻¹. The large moss RCS makes the interpolation weakly sensitive to turgor and keeps resistance near its prescribed surface value. This explains how a function named “stomatal” can still be called for moss without implying active vascular-style stomatal control.

Source: PlantMathFuncMod.F90:555

Botanical context: this is a representation of vegetative moss physiology. Some real moss sporophytes have stomata; an experiment on 11 species found their behavior differs from vascular stomata. The model inspected here does not explicitly distinguish gametophyte and sporophyte gas exchange. Kubásek et al. (2021).

06 · Allocation and phenology

Shared carbon machinery, different drying and turnover rules

Plant C/N/P is carried through structural tissues, nonstructural reserves, growth respiration, maintenance respiration, senescence and litterfall. Both groups use branches and nodes. “Seed,” “leaf,” “stalk” and “root” labels are shared mathematical compartments; moss establishment should be interpreted as propagule/reserve initialization, not the planting of botanical grass seeds.

Source: InitPlantMod.F90:1247 · biocrust_pft_mgmt.nc.cdl:38

Drying can reduce maintenance as well as growth

Moss shoot maintenance respiration is multiplied by WaterStress4Groth. Root-like maintenance has an analogous moisture multiplier. Grass receives this maintenance reduction only when the relevant phenology condition also selects the drought-deciduous branch; it is not universal for every grass. The expansion-to-respiration mapping also differs: fRespWatSens(W)=W^0.10 for moss and W^0.25 otherwise.

Source: PlantBranchMod.F90:3180 · RootMod.F90:678 · PlantMathFuncMod.F90:333

This can reduce maintenance demand during desiccation. It does not independently prove complete dormancy, survival, or a realistic rewetting lag: those require the biomass, phenology and reserve trajectories.

Phenology uses a separate botanical threshold

PSIMin4LeafOff(IEBTYP) is −200 MPa for botanical bryophytes and −2 MPa for the other listed botanical classes. The source compares this array to canopy potential and accumulates low-ψ hours; the array comment’s “[h]” unit is inconsistent with that comparison. Treat the values as MPa thresholds in a phenology pathway, not measured lethal water potentials. The final response also depends on the chosen phenology rules.

Source: PlantPhenolMod.F90:29 · PlantPhenolMod.F90:159

A current reader guard rejects drought-leaf-out combinations IWTYP=2,4,5 when IEBTYP lies outside 2–4. Therefore botanical moss (IEBTYP=0) cannot simply be assigned those drought-leaf-out settings in this revision.

Source: PlantInfoMod.F90:601

Nutrients and N₂ fixation

Both groups participate in the plant uptake/respiration framework and compete for soil resources. N₂ fixation is an explicit configuration choice: INTYP=0 disables it, 1–3 select root-like symbiosis, and 4–6 select canopy symbiosis. For canopy fixation the model converts carbon oxidation into fixed N, constrained by bacterial N demand and available growth respiration: N fixed = C respired for fixation × EN2F. Moss is not automatically assigned a free nitrogen source.

Source: ElmIDMod.F90:117 · NoduleBGCMod.F90:94 · NoduleBGCMod.F90:199

07 · After tissue death

Different litter chemistry feeds the same decomposition framework

Dead material enters shared litter and microbial decomposition pools. The initialization routines assign these carbon fractions to foliar litter:

Sum100%100%

Source: InitPlantMod.F90:243 · StartqMod.F90:228

The moss foliar, nonfoliar, stalk and root-like litter branches all use 7/25/30/38 in the reviewed initialization code. Other grass organs use different fractions, and N-fixing plants can select another foliar branch. The category called ilignin is a model kinetic allocation; these fractions should not be reported as laboratory chemical measurements.

Both moss and non-woody grasses route fallen standing-dead material into the surface fine-litter complex in the shared growth routine. Differences in turnover and pool chemistry can change litter persistence, but a larger resistant-pool fraction alone does not determine net litter accumulation. Moisture, temperature, microbial activity, inputs and disturbance transfers also matter.

Source: GrosubsMod.F90:204

Living moss ≠ surface litter. The moss canopy is a plant PFT. Its dead material contributes to litter. Surf_LitrC_col and PSI_LITR_col do not isolate living moss biomass or its hydration.

10 · Interpretation

What this representation can support

Established by this code trace

  • Explicit nonvascular switches embedded in a shared plant model.
  • Different canopy-ψ sensitivity, gas-resistance treatment, root sink weighting and litter carbon partitioning.
  • Configurable life cycle, fixation, porosity, morphology and photosynthetic traits.
  • Separate living canopy, canopy-held water, tissue water and dead litter states.

Requires additional evidence

  • Species-specific moss desiccation tolerance and reactivation kinetics.
  • Realism of effective “root” uptake depth and any deep-soil access.
  • A dedicated vertically resolved living moss mat, Sphagnum storage anatomy or wet-film CO₂ diffusion response; none is established by the shared pathway alone.
  • Moss versus grass GPP, ET, biomass or drought survival under matched forcing and initialization.

Model names and flags are proxies. For a scientific comparison, first check the selected records, initial reserve C, population density, depth and soil layering; then compare matched runs and measured hydration/fluxes. The biocrust example uses lich22 and moss22 through the plant-management system. This file is an explanation, not a new simulation or a calibrated parameter recommendation.

Source: biocrust_pft_mgmt.nc.cdl:38 · InitPlantMod.F90:1247

Research context

The ecosys peatland study of Mezbahuddin, Grant & Flanagan (2016) describes nonvascular evaporation using a fixed surface resistance while retaining shared soil–plant hydraulics. It also explains how carbon supply, competition and oxygen limitations produce shallow moss uptake. That supports the conceptual interpretation here; the local EcoSIM source remains authoritative for this revision.

Kubásek et al. (2021) provides experimental context for moss sporophyte gas exchange. Its results should not be treated as direct calibration of the canopy or phenology coefficients above.
