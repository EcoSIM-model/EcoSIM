Canopy radiation modeling

EcoSIM distributes direct and diffuse shortwave radiation through exposed leaf, live-stem and standing-dead layers. Retained canopy snow changes their optical properties and adds interception area. The same snow-added areas determine the bulk canopy/ground longwave partition.

Hourly sequence and retained snow

1 · Physical geometryRead current PFT traits and retained stores; calculate exposed live and dead areas.
2 · Snow opticsDerive snow coverage and added area from each PFT’s retained SWE and current exposed geometry.
3 · RadiationRun multilayer shortwave transfer and derive longwave interception weights from the same snow-added areas.
4 · Water and energyIntercept new precipitation, unload snow, and solve the canopy/ground energy and water balances.

PlantAPICanMSend supplies the retained per-PFT SnowOnCanopy_pft and the input snow-interception class. UpdateOpticalProperties derives current snow cover after exposed geometry is available. Radiation therefore does not depend on the preceding hour’s exposed/buried classification or on a column output accumulator cleared at hour entry.

The radiation calculation uses the retained store at radiation entry. Current-hour interception and unloading follow radiation in HOUR1; their accepted store changes affect subsequent radiation. This order prevents current precipitation or unloading from being applied twice. The radiation preparation reads water stocks and changes optical geometry; it does not transfer water or add canopy heat capacity.

Physical area plus snow-added area

For PFT i, let Ai be its exposed physical leaf, live-stem and dead-stem area; Vs,i its retained intercepted snow water-equivalent volume; and bi its snow-area coefficient.

Ai = Alive,i + Adead,i   [m²/cell]
ΔAsnow,i = bi Vs,i   [m²/cell]
Arad,i = Ai + ΔAsnow,i
mi = Arad,i / Ai = 1 + biVs,i/Ai,   Ai > 0

The dimensionless multiplier mi applies equally to the exposed leaf, live-stem and dead-stem sector arrays. Their physical proportions and vertical/angle distributions are retained. A PFT with zero exposed area has zero radiative area even if buried material retains snow. Physical geometry remains the basis for precipitation capacity, biomass heat capacity and aerodynamic cover.

Shared parameters CanopySnowCapacityPerArea and CanopySnowAreaCoeff are indexed by the isnowcept_* enum. CanopySnowOptics computes both the radiative area and the snow-covered fraction:

fs,i = min[1, (Vs,i / (cs,iAi))0.15],   Ai > 0
fs,i = 0 and Arad,i = 0,   Ai = 0

Multiplying cell area, physical canopy areas and snow volume by the same factor multiplies Arad,i by that factor, while snow coverage, area multipliers and radiation fractions remain unchanged. For example, A = 654.803 m², Vs = 0.606838 m³ and b = 100 m²/m³ give ΔAsnow = 60.684 m² and m = 1.092675.

Direct, diffuse and scattered shortwave

WthrMod partitions the incoming horizontal shortwave into direct beam and diffuse sky components using solar geometry. Internal radiation intensities are in MJ m⁻² h⁻¹; multiplying W m⁻² by 0.0036 gives this unit. Leaf/stem area converts those intensities into cell-integrated absorbed energies in MJ h⁻¹.

UpdateOpticalProperties mixes leaf, live-stem and dead-stem reflectance/transmittance with canopy-snow optics using fs,i. It applies the inherited 20% snow-cover reduction of leaf clumping and distributes snow-added area using mi. Snow optical constants are shortwave albedo 0.8, PAR albedo 0.95 and zero snow transmittance.

AngularDistributeRadiation resolves beam incidence by leaf inclination and azimuth. DownwellSweep attenuates direct/diffuse light from canopy top downward and records absorption, reflection and transmission. RadiationAtGround sets the lower reflection boundary. UpwellSweep then adds absorption of upward scattered light. PFT and layer sums become RadSWCanopyAbsorption_pft and RadSWCanopyLAbsroption_pft; RadSWGrnd_col is incident ground shortwave.

The multilayer shortwave solver computes its own absorption. The Beer-law weights below are the bulk geometry used for longwave partition and existing interception interfaces; they are not substituted for the multilayer shortwave transfer. In radiation-only diagnostic mode, ground shortwave retains the existing unshaded treatment.

Column optical depth and PFT longwave shares

CanopyRadiationFractions sums the same PFT radiative areas used by shortwave optics, including PFT-specific snow coefficients.

Arad,col = Σ Arad,i
LAIeff = Arad,col / Aground
f̄snow = Σ(fs,iArad,i) / Arad,col
Fcanopy = 1 − exp[−(0.65 + 0.15 f̄snow) LAIeff]
Fi = Fcanopy Arad,i / Arad,col
Flive,i = Fi Alive,i / Ai
Fground = 1 − Fcanopy

Zero-area PFTs receive zero shares; an empty or completely buried column has Fground = 1. The column partition satisfies ΣFi + Fground = 1. The total/live PFT shares populate FracPARads2Canopy_pft and FracPARads2LiveCanopy_pft, retaining those existing interface names.

Ki = εcanopy σ Fi Aground sOTC
Rn,i = SWabs,i + (LWsky + LWground)Fi − 2KiTi⁴
∂Rn,i/∂Ti = −8KiTi³

The factor two represents the two emitting sides in the canopy energy formulation. One-side canopy emission is accumulated for downward longwave to the ground. Live and passive canopy energy solvers use these same radiative definitions.

Snow burial, thermal mass and aerodynamic exchange

Full-model exposure uses the existing whole-canopy-layer snowline test; prescribed phenology uses its existing canopy-profile exposure calculation. Buried tissue and retained water/snow keep their thermal inventory. These optical calculations do not introduce a fractional moving-snowline model or conductive exchange between buried canopy tissue and snow.

Radiative area and turbulent projected exchange area have different purposes. Snow-added radiative area changes interception and emission weights; it is not used to multiply physical material, water stores or the turbulent exchange area.

Ri = clamp[RIB(Tair − Tcanopy), −0.3, 0.075]
rH₂O,target = max[rmin, rneutral/(1 − 10Ri)]
rheat = rH₂O/0.92

CanopyVaporResistance supplies the common stability target. The live coupled solve damps resistance updates to 0.9–1.1 times the previous iteration value while approaching that target. The passive scalar energy solve evaluates the target directly at each trial temperature. The 10% iteration limiter is not a separate physical bound on passive exchange.
